🎯 Anek shabdon ke liye ek shabd — Hindi one-word substitution

Saying a long phrase in one word — “jo Ishwar mein vishwaas rakhe” (one who believes in God) = aastik. Here are 60 important examples with English meanings.

To make language tight, one word replaces a whole phrase: “jo kabhi na mare” (one who never dies) becomes “amar”, “jise jeeta na ja sake” becomes “ajey” (invincible). Good essays, letters and answers use these to sound short and polished.

A few patterns make many of them easy: “-neey” means worthy of (darshaneey = worth seeing, poojaneey, vishwasaneey); “a-” means not (ajey, adrishya, amar); “sarva-” means all (sarvagya, sarvavyaapi); “-char” means one that moves or lives (jalchar, nabhchar, thalchar); “-ik” for time (dainik, maasik, vaarshik).

The one word must carry the full meaning. For “one who cannot read or write”, “niraksar” or “anpadh” is right but “agyaani” is not — agyaani means lacking knowledge, not lacking schooling. Many come in pairs (aastik/naastik, kritagya/kritaghn); learn them together.

Easy ways to learn them

  • Master the patterns (-neey, a-, sarva-, -char, -ik) first.
  • Learn pairs together: agraj–anuj, sulabh–durlabh, paraadheen–swaadheen.
  • Play the quiz below every day.

Frequently asked questions

One word for “one who believes in God”?

Aastik; the opposite is naastik.

One word for “that which has no end”?

Anant.

One word for “one who knows everything”?

Sarvagya.

One word for “one who does not acknowledge a favour”?

Kritaghn; one who does is kritagya.
