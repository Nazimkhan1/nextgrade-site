How income tax works in FY 2026-27

Income earned from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 (tax year 2026-27) is taxed under one of two regimes. The new regime applies by default; you may opt for the old regime instead. The Budget of 1 February 2026 did not change the slabs — the FY 2025-26 rates continue. From 1 April 2026 the new Income-tax Act, 2025 is in force; it uses “tax year” instead of “assessment year” and renumbers sections, but the rates are the same.

The new regime has lower rates but allows few deductions (no 80C, HRA or home-loan interest). The old regime has higher rates but allows those deductions. The right choice depends on your deductions — the calculator above works out both side by side.

Tax slabs and rules (FY 2026-27)

New regime: up to ₹4 lakh — nil; ₹4–8 lakh — 5%; ₹8–12 lakh — 10%; ₹12–16 lakh — 15%; ₹16–20 lakh — 20%; ₹20–24 lakh — 25%; above ₹24 lakh — 30%. Standard deduction ₹75,000 on salary/pension. If taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh, the whole tax is rebated (up to ₹60,000). Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief keeps the tax from exceeding the income above ₹12 lakh.

Old regime: up to ₹2.5 lakh — nil (₹3 lakh at 60+, ₹5 lakh at 80+); up to ₹5 lakh — 5%; ₹5–10 lakh — 20%; above ₹10 lakh — 30%. Standard deduction ₹50,000; rebate up to ₹12,500 if taxable income is up to ₹5 lakh. Deductions such as 80C (max ₹1.5 lakh), 80D, HRA and home-loan interest (max ₹2 lakh) are subtracted.

Both: surcharge 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore (37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime only); then 4% health and education cess on tax plus surcharge.

Examples

Salary ₹12,75,000 (new regime): minus ₹75,000 standard deduction = taxable ₹12,00,000. Slab tax is ₹60,000, fully rebated — total tax ₹0.

Salary ₹15,00,000: new regime taxable ₹14,25,000, tax ₹93,750 + cess = ₹97,500 (about ₹8,125 a month). Old regime with ₹1.5 lakh 80C and ₹25,000 80D: taxable ₹12,75,000, total tax ₹2,02,800 — the new regime is cheaper here.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there no tax up to ₹12 lakh?

In the new regime, if taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh, the section 87A rebate (carried into the new Act) cancels the whole tax, up to ₹60,000. Salaried people also get the ₹75,000 standard deduction, so a salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no tax. The rebate does not cover income taxed at special rates, such as capital gains.

Which is better, the new or the old regime?

If your deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest) are small, the new regime is usually cheaper. With large deductions — say a big HRA plus a home loan — the old regime can win. Enter your deductions above to compare.

Did Budget 2026 change the tax slabs?

No. The Budget of 1 February 2026 kept the personal tax slabs, rebate and standard deduction of FY 2025-26. The new Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026.

Is this enough to file my return?

No, it is an estimate. Before filing, check your Form 16/AIS on incometax.gov.in or ask a tax professional. (Rules checked: 27 November 2026)
