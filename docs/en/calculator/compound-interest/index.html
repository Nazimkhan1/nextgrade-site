📊 Compound Interest Calculator

Enter the principal, rate, time and how often interest is added, to find the compound interest and total amount.

What is compound interest

In compound interest, the interest of each period is added to the principal, and next time interest is charged on this larger amount — that is, "interest on interest". Over a long time it grows much more than simple interest, which is why it is so powerful for savings and investment.

Formula

Amount A = P × (1 + r ÷ (100 × n))^(n × t). Compound interest = A − P. Here P = principal, r = yearly rate, t = years, n = how many times a year interest is added.

Example

₹10,000 at 8% per year for 3 years, compounded yearly: A = 10000 × (1.08)³ ≈ ₹12,597. So the compound interest ≈ ₹2,597 (a little more than the ₹2,400 simple interest).

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate compound interest?

A = P × (1 + r/(100n))^(nt), and CI = A − P. This calculator does the maths for you.

What does "quarterly compounding" mean?

It means interest is added every 3 months (4 times a year). Bank FDs are often compounded quarterly.

Why is compound interest more than simple interest?

Because the earlier interest also earns interest later, so the amount grows faster over time.
