🇮🇳 Republic Day (26 January) speech and essay

Easy Republic Day speeches for school students — short, medium and long — with an essay and 10 lines. Tap "Listen" to practise, or print it.

All about the day

How to begin your speech

Respected Principal, honourable teachers and my dear friends, a very happy Republic Day to all of you.

I thank you all for giving me the chance to speak a few words on this proud occasion.

Short speech (1 minute) — Classes 1 to 4

Respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends, happy Republic Day to all of you.

Today is 26 January. On 26 January 1950, the Constitution of India came into force. The Constitution has the rules that run our country. From that day, India became a republic.

On this day we hoist the tricolour, sing the national anthem and remember the brave people of our country.

Let us promise to study well, love everyone and make our country proud. Jai Hind! Jai Bharat!

Medium speech (2–3 minutes) — Classes 5 to 8

Respected Principal, honourable teachers and my dear friends, warm wishes to all of you on the 78th Republic Day.

India became free on 15 August 1947, but the rules for running the country were not ready yet. A Constituent Assembly was formed and worked for nearly three years to prepare our Constitution. Dr B. R. Ambedkar chaired its Drafting Committee. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. On that day Dr Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India.

26 January was chosen because on 26 January 1930 the nation had pledged itself to "Purna Swaraj" — complete independence.

Our Constitution gives us equality, freedom and the right to speak our mind. It also gives us duties — to follow the law, respect everyone and protect public property.

On this day a grand parade takes place on Kartavya Path in New Delhi, with soldiers, tableaux from the states and a flypast.

Let us resolve today to be good citizens, keep our surroundings clean and do our part for the nation's progress. Jai Hind!

Long speech (4–5 minutes) — Classes 9 to 12

Respected Principal, honoured guests, teachers and my dear friends, warm greetings to all of you on the 78th Republic Day.

Today is a day of pride for every Indian. On 26 January 1950 our Constitution came into force and India became a sovereign, democratic republic — a country where the people choose their own government, and the head of the state, the President, comes from an election and not from a royal family.

After independence, the Constituent Assembly debated and worked for about 2 years, 11 months and 18 days to create the longest written constitution in the world. Dr B. R. Ambedkar, who chaired the Drafting Committee, is called the chief architect of the Constitution. It was adopted on 26 November 1949 — which is why 26 November is celebrated as Constitution Day — and came into force on 26 January 1950.

The Preamble begins with "We, the people of India". These words tell us that the real strength of this nation is its people. The Preamble promises us justice, liberty, equality and fraternity.

The Constitution gives us Fundamental Rights — the right to equality, freedom of speech and the right to education. Along with rights come Fundamental Duties: to respect the Constitution and national symbols, to protect the unity of the country, to protect the environment and to develop a scientific temper.

As we watch the parade on Kartavya Path, let us remember the freedom fighters and soldiers because of whom we are free and safe today.

Friends, a nation is made not just by its borders but by its citizens. If we study honestly, follow the law, treat no one unfairly and keep our surroundings clean, that is true patriotism.

So on this Republic Day, let us resolve to live the values of our Constitution not only in books but in our everyday lives. Jai Hind! Jai Bharat!

Essay: Republic Day (about 250 words)

Republic Day is a national festival of India, celebrated every year on 26 January. On this day in 1950 our Constitution came into force and India became a republic.

India became independent on 15 August 1947, but it needed its own Constitution to run the country. The Constituent Assembly, with the Drafting Committee chaired by Dr B. R. Ambedkar, prepared the Constitution, which was adopted on 26 November 1949. It came into force on 26 January 1950, and Dr Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India. The date 26 January was chosen because on this day in 1930 the pledge of Purna Swaraj was taken.

On this day the President unfurls the national flag in New Delhi and a grand parade is held on Kartavya Path. Contingents of the Army, Navy and Air Force, tableaux from different states and colourful programmes by children are part of it. Gallantry awards are also presented. The celebrations end on 29 January with the "Beating Retreat" ceremony.

In schools, offices, villages and cities, the flag is hoisted, the national anthem is sung and patriotic programmes are held.

Republic Day reminds us that the Constitution has given us rights, and that we also have duties. We should respect the Constitution, live in harmony and contribute to the progress of the nation. That is the real message of this festival.

10 lines on Republic Day

  1. Republic Day is celebrated every year on 26 January.
  2. The Constitution of India came into force on 26 January 1950.
  3. On this day India became a republic.
  4. Dr Rajendra Prasad was the first President of India.
  5. Dr B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constitution.
  6. It is our national festival.
  7. A grand parade is held on Kartavya Path in New Delhi.
  8. In schools the flag is hoisted and the national anthem is sung.
  9. The celebrations end with Beating Retreat on 29 January.
  10. This day reminds us of our rights and our duties.

Slogans and closing lines

  • Jai Hind, Jai Bharat!
  • Respect the Constitution — it is India's pride.
  • Rights and duties together make a true republic.
  • One nation, one tricolour, one resolve — India moves ahead.

Easy tips for giving the speech

  • Practise reading it aloud 3–4 times — you can also listen with the "Listen" button.
  • Speak slowly and clearly, and look at the audience now and then.
  • Learn the dates and names well (26 January 1950, Dr Ambedkar, Dr Rajendra Prasad).
  • End with a loud, proud "Jai Hind".

Frequently asked questions

Why is Republic Day celebrated on 26 January?

Because the Constitution of India came into force on 26 January 1950. The date was chosen because the pledge of Purna Swaraj was taken on 26 January 1930.

Which Republic Day is it in 2027?

On 26 January 2027 India celebrates its 78th Republic Day.

Which speech is best for small children?

For Classes 1 to 4, the short (1-minute) speech above is best — simple words and the key facts.
