⚪ How to play Nau Gutti — full rules

Nau Gutti is an old two-player game played on a board of three squares, drawn on a village platform, in a courtyard or carved into temple stone. Each player has 9 pieces, and three pieces in a line take away one of the opponent's. Here are the full rules in simple words.

▶️ Play online

What you need

  • A board drawn with chalk or charcoal on the ground, or on paper: three squares one inside the other, with the middle points of their sides joined by straight lines (24 points in all)
  • 9 matching pieces for one player — pebbles, for example
  • 9 different-looking pieces for the other player — tamarind seeds or cowrie shells, for example
  • 2 players

How to play, step by step

  1. Toss to decide who starts. The board begins empty.
  2. Part one — placing: take turns putting one piece on any empty point until both players have placed all 9.
  3. Whenever three of a player's pieces stand on one straight line (along one side, or along a joining line), that is a "mill". The player who made it takes one of the opponent's pieces off the board.
  4. A piece that is part of a mill cannot be taken — unless every piece the opponent has is in a mill.
  5. Part two — moving: once all pieces are placed, on each turn slide one of your pieces along a line to the next empty point. Jumping over pieces is not allowed.
  6. If you open one of your mills by moving a piece out and close it again on a later turn, it counts as a new mill and you take another piece.
  7. Part three — flying: in many places a player who is down to 3 pieces may move a piece to any empty point. In many villages this rule is not played — agree on it before you start.

Winning, losing and draws

A player left with only 2 pieces loses — they can no longer make a mill.

A player who cannot move any piece on their turn (all are boxed in) also loses.

If no piece is taken for a long time, or the same position keeps coming back, the players can agree on a draw.

Other names and history

It is called Nau Gutti, Nau Goti, Navkankari and in some places Char Bhar; "Noy Guti" in Bengali and Nine Men's Morris in English. Boards like this are found carved in the stone of old temples, forts and buildings in other countries. How old the game is and where it began are questions experts disagree on. Some places also play a version with diagonal lines at the corners and 12 pieces each.

Easy tips to play better

  • The four crossing points of the middle square, where four paths meet, are the most useful points early on.
  • If your opponent has two pieces in a line, block the third point first.
  • While placing, spread your pieces so they still have room to move later.
  • Try to build a "grinder", where moving one piece back and forth closes a mill every turn.

Frequently asked questions

How many pieces are there in Nau Gutti?

9 for each player, 18 in all, on a board with 24 points.

Which piece can I take after making a mill?

Any opponent piece that is not part of a mill. If all their pieces are in mills, you may take any of them.

Is flying with 3 pieces a must?

No, it is a local rule. Agree on it with the other player before the game starts.
