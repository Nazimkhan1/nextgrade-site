🐚 How to play Chaupar / Pachisi — full rules

Chaupar and Pachisi are an old Indian board game played on a cross-shaped cloth board with cowrie shells or long dice. Each player races their pieces all the way round the board and back into the central home (char-koni). Here are the common rules in simple words — they vary a little from place to place.

▶️ Play online

What you need

  • A cross-shaped board (often embroidered cloth): four arms of 3 rows × 8 squares around a central home
  • 4 pieces per player in different colours (often red, yellow, green and black)
  • 6 cowrie shells (7 in some places) — or 3 long dice for Chaupar
  • 2 or 4 players; with 4, players sitting opposite are partners

How to play, step by step

  1. Each player sits in front of one arm of the board. Turns go anticlockwise.
  2. On your turn throw 6 cowries and count how many land mouth up: 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 open shells move that many squares; 1 open shell is 10; none open is 25.
  3. 10 and 25 are called graces. A grace lets a new piece enter the board, and you get another throw.
  4. A piece first travels outward down the middle row of its own arm, then goes anticlockwise all round the outer squares of the board.
  5. After the full circuit the piece comes back up the middle row of its own arm into the central home. Usually you need the exact number to go in.
  6. If your piece stops exactly on a square with an opponent's piece, that piece is captured and must start again. In many places a capture earns another throw.
  7. The squares marked with a cross are "castles" — a piece standing there cannot be captured.
  8. In a partner game you never capture your partner, and a player whose pieces are all home can help the partner.

Who wins

The first player to bring all their pieces into the central home wins.

In a partner game the team wins when both partners have all their pieces home.

Other names

It is called Chaupar, Chausar, Pachisi and Pachchisi, and Pasha or Pochishi in Bengal. "Pachisi" comes from pachchis, 25 — the biggest throw of the cowries. Ludo is a simplified form of this old game.

For a good game

  • Agree on the local rules before you start: which throws are graces, whether the exact number is needed to go home, and whether a capture gives another throw.
  • Throw the cowries on cloth or a mat so they do not break.
  • Keep cowries away from small children's mouths.
  • Play only for fun — no bets or money.

Frequently asked questions

How do you count the cowries in Pachisi?

Count how many of the 6 shells land mouth up: that many squares for 2 to 6. One open shell is 10, none open is 25. 10 and 25 are graces.

What is the difference between Chaupar and Pachisi?

The board is the same. Pachisi uses cowrie shells and Chaupar usually uses 3 long dice. In many places both names are used for the same game.

What are the castle squares?

The 12 squares marked with a cross on the board, where a standing piece cannot be captured.
