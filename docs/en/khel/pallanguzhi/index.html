🌰 How to play Pallanguzhi — full rules

Pallanguzhi is an old seed-sowing game from South India, played by two people on a long wooden board. There is no luck in it, only counting and planning. Here are the rules of one common way to play, in simple words — every region plays it a little differently.

▶️ Play online

What you need

  • A Pallanguzhi board — two rows of 7 pits (14 in all)
  • No board? 14 small pits in the ground or 14 small bowls
  • 70 seeds — tamarind seeds, cowrie shells, manjadi seeds or small pebbles
  • 2 players, facing each other

How to play, step by step

  1. Put the board between the two players. The row of 7 pits on your side is yours. Put 5 seeds in every pit (many families use 6 or more).
  2. Decide who starts — the first move is an advantage, so take turns starting each game.
  3. On your turn, choose a pit on your side that has seeds and lift all of them.
  4. Going anticlockwise, drop one seed into each of the following pits — the other player's pits included.
  5. When the last seed falls, look at the next pit. If it has seeds, lift them and keep sowing the same way.
  6. If the next pit is empty, all the seeds in the pit after it are yours — keep them by your side. Your turn ends.
  7. If two empty pits come one after the other, you get nothing and the turn passes.
  8. Pasu (a rule many families play): whenever a pit reaches exactly 4 seeds during sowing, those 4 seeds go to the owner of that pit.

Rounds and winning

When the player to move has no seeds on their side, the round ends. The other player takes the seeds left on their own side. Whoever has more seeds wins the round.

In a full game, before the next round each player refills their own pits with 5 seeds each from what they won. A pit that cannot be filled stays closed for that round. A player who cannot fill even one pit loses.

If near the end the seeds just go round and round and nobody wins any, agree to end the round — each player takes the seeds on their side.

Other names

Pallanguzhi in Tamil Nadu, Ali Guli Mane in Karnataka, Vamana Guntalu in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Close relatives are played in Kerala and Sri Lanka. Seed-sowing games like this are known around the world as the "mancala" family.

Keep in mind

  • With small children, use big seeds or shells and make sure nothing goes into the mouth.
  • Count before you move to see where the last seed will land.
  • Agree on the rules (how many seeds, pasu or not) before you start.
  • Count the seeds back into the box after playing so the set stays complete.

Frequently asked questions

How many seeds go in each pit in Pallanguzhi?

Usually 5 or 6, but some families use up to 12, and in some places the middle pit gets a different number. Agree before you play.

How do you win seeds in Pallanguzhi?

If the pit after your last seed is empty, all seeds in the pit after that are yours. With the pasu rule, a pit that reaches 4 seeds also goes to its owner.

What is the difference between Ali Guli Mane and Pallanguzhi?

Broadly they are the same game — Ali Guli Mane is the Kannada name and Pallanguzhi the Tamil one. Small rules change from place to place.
