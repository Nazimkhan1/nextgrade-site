📜 Rules of desi games

How to play India's traditional games — what you need, step-by-step rules, outs and winning — in simple words, with a free online version of each game.

🎯

How to play Carrom — full rules

Carrom is the board game found in almost every Indian home. You flick a striker with your finger to send your coins into the four corner pockets. Here are the full rules in simple words.

🔵

How to play Kanche (Marbles) — full rules

Kanche — colourful glass marbles — is one of the oldest street games in India. There are many ways to play; here are the two most popular, the pit game and the circle game, in simple words.

🏏

How to play Gilli-Danda — full rules

Gilli-danda is an old village and street game that many call "desi cricket". A long stick (danda) is used to flick a small wooden piece (gilli) into the air and hit it far. Here are the rules in simple words.

🪨

How to play Pitthu (Lagori / Seven Stones) — full rules

Pitthu is a team game played with seven flat stones and a ball. One team knocks down the pile and tries to rebuild it, while the other team tries to stop them by hitting them with the ball. Here are the rules in simple words.

⚪

How to play Nau Gutti — full rules

Nau Gutti is an old two-player game played on a board of three squares, drawn on a village platform, in a courtyard or carved into temple stone. Each player has 9 pieces, and three pieces in a line take away one of the opponent's. Here are the full rules in simple words.

🐚

How to play Chaupar / Pachisi — full rules

Chaupar and Pachisi are an old Indian board game played on a cross-shaped cloth board with cowrie shells or long dice. Each player races their pieces all the way round the board and back into the central home (char-koni). Here are the common rules in simple words — they vary a little from place to place.

🔴

How to play Sholo Guti — full rules

Sholo Guti ("sixteen pieces" in Bengali) is an old two-player village game played on lines drawn on the ground, with pebbles or seeds as pieces. Each side has 16 pieces and captures by jumping over the other side's pieces. Here are the full rules in simple words.

🌰

How to play Pallanguzhi — full rules

Pallanguzhi is an old seed-sowing game from South India, played by two people on a long wooden board. There is no luck in it, only counting and planning. Here are the rules of one common way to play, in simple words — every region plays it a little differently.
