Old and interesting facts about the Islamic calendar

  1. A lunar year has about 354-355 days, roughly 11 days shorter than the solar year. That is why Ramadan and Eid come about 11 days earlier every year, and in about 33 years Ramadan passes through every season.
  2. The Hijri calendar was set in the time of the second Caliph, Hazrat Umar (RA), around 638 CE. Year 1 is the year of the Hijrah (622 CE), when the Prophet ﷺ moved from Makkah to Madinah.
  3. The month names follow seasons (spring, dry, heat) because months were once kept in step with the seasons by adding an extra month now and then (nasi'). Islam ended this (Surah At-Tawbah 9:36-37), so the months now move through all the seasons.
  4. There are four sacred months: Muharram, Rajab, Dhu al-Qa'dah and Dhu al-Hijjah.
  5. In old times Rajab was called "al-Asamm", "the deaf", because no clash of weapons was heard in it.
  6. An Islamic date begins at Maghrib (sunset). So the "night" of Shab-e-Barat or Laylat al-Qadr starts on the evening before the date.
  7. Friday's old Arabic name was "Yawm al-'Arubah". The word "Jumu'ah" comes from "gathering".
  8. Some people think Safar is unlucky, but in a hadith the Prophet ﷺ said "La Safar" — there is no bad omen in Safar.
