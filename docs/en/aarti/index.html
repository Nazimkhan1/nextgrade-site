🪔 Aarti lyrics with meaning

The eight aartis sung most in Indian homes — full lyrics in Devanagari and English letters, the meaning of every verse, bigger text and print.

The word “aarti” is thought to come from the Sanskrit “aaratrika” — waving a lamp at night to see the deity in its light. Over time it became the devotional song sung at the end of a puja. Temples hold several aartis a day, from the morning Mangala aarti to the night Shayan aarti; homes usually do one in the morning and one in the evening.

All the aartis here come from a centuries-old tradition and belong to everyone. “Om Jai Jagdish Hare” was written by Pandit Shardha Ram Phillauri in the 19th century; the Shiva and Ambe aartis name Swami Shivanand in their last verse; the others come from folk and temple tradition. The lyrics follow the common printed reading, and the meaning of each verse is written by us in plain words so that even children understand what they are singing.

How to do aarti — the simple way

Aarti is the last part of a puja. Keep a ghee lamp or camphor on a plate, with a few flowers, rice (akshat), roli and a bell. After the puja, reading or food offering, everyone stands and sings the aarti.

The lamp plate is moved clockwise in front of the idol or picture. One common way: four times before the feet, twice before the navel, once before the face, then seven times around the whole form. Every family has its own custom — what your home does is right.

After the aarti, people pass their hands over the flame and touch their eyes and head, and then prasad is shared. Mind children, curtains and fan breeze around the lamp and camphor; a camphor flame is strong.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the most sung aarti?

“Om Jai Jagdish Hare” — it closes almost every puja.

In what order are aartis sung?

Usually Ganesh first, then the deity of the day, and “Om Jai Jagdish Hare” at the end.

How many wicks should the lamp have?

One, five or seven wicks are common; camphor is also used. Devotion matters more than the number.

Can aarti be done sitting down?

People usually stand, but elderly or unwell people can sit.
