🌙 Roza Duas — Sehri Niyat and Iftar Dua

Intention (niyat) for the fast at Sehri

Reference: The commonly recited wording — the niyat is really the intention in the heart; these words are not from a hadith

وَبِصَوْمِ غَدٍ نَوَيْتُ مِنْ شَهْرِ رَمَضَانَ

Wa bi-sawmi ghadin nawaytu min shahri Ramadan

I intend to keep tomorrow’s fast of the month of Ramadan.

Iftar dua (the most widely recited)

Reference: The short form “Allahumma laka sumtu wa ‘ala rizqika aftartu” is in Sunan Abu Dawud; the longer form is widely printed in South Asia

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي لَكَ صُمْتُ وَبِكَ آمَنْتُ

Allahumma inni laka sumtu wa bika amantu

O Allah, for You I fasted and in You I believe,

وَعَلَيْكَ تَوَكَّلْتُ وَعَلَىٰ رِزْقِكَ أَفْطَرْتُ

wa ‘alayka tawakkaltu wa ‘ala rizqika aftartu

in You I put my trust, and with Your provision I break my fast.

Dua after breaking the fast (Dhahaba-z-zama’u)

Reference: Sunan Abu Dawud — the Prophet ﷺ said this on breaking his fast

ذَهَبَ الظَّمَأُ وَابْتَلَّتِ الْعُرُوقُ وَثَبَتَ الْأَجْرُ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ

Dhahaba-z-zama’u wabtallatil-‘uruqu wa thabatal-ajru in sha’ Allah

The thirst is gone, the veins are moist again, and the reward is sure, if Allah wills.

Dua for Laylatul Qadr (Shab-e-Qadr)

Reference: Jami at-Tirmidhi — taught by the Prophet ﷺ to Aisha (RA)

اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي

Allahumma innaka ‘afuwwun tuhibbul-‘afwa fa‘fu ‘anni

O Allah, You are the One who pardons, and You love to pardon — so pardon me.

About these duas

Two moments shape every day of Ramadan — Sehri (suhoor), when the fast begins, and Iftar, when it is broken at sunset. This page gives the duas most often recited at both times, in Arabic, in English letters and with a plain English meaning, so that anyone who cannot read Arabic can still say them correctly and know what they are asking for.

A word about the niyat (intention): the intention is in the heart. If you eat sehri meaning to fast tomorrow, your intention is made. The words “Wa bi-sawmi ghadin…” are commonly taught to children in South Asia, but they are not from a hadith — the fast is valid even if you do not say them aloud.

Two duas are well known for iftar. “Allahumma laka sumtu…” is recited in almost every home; its short form is reported in Sunan Abu Dawud. The second, “Dhahaba-z-zama’u…”, is reported as what the Prophet ﷺ said when he broke his fast — so many people break the fast with a date or water and then say it. Saying both is fine.

Laylatul Qadr is sought in the last ten nights of Ramadan, especially the odd nights (21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th, 29th). When Aisha (RA) asked what to say on that night, the Prophet ﷺ taught her “Allahumma innaka ‘afuwwun…”. It is short and easy for children to learn.

How to make dua — the simple way

  • Begin by praising Allah (e.g. “Alhamdulillah”) and sending durood on the Prophet ﷺ.
  • Raise your hands and ask with full conviction and attention — a dua from the heart is the most precious.
  • If you do not know Arabic, you may ask in your own language — Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, English; Allah understands every language.
  • Do not be hasty or say “my dua was not answered”; be patient and keep asking.
  • Pray for your parents, family and the whole ummah along with yourself.

Frequently asked questions

When is the iftar dua recited?

At sunset, while or just after breaking the fast. “Dhahaba-z-zama’u…” is said after taking a date or water.

Must the intention for fasting be spoken?

No. The niyat is the intention in the heart; saying it aloud is not required, though it does no harm.

What is the dua for Laylatul Qadr?

“Allahumma innaka ‘afuwwun tuhibbul-‘afwa fa‘fu ‘anni” — O Allah, You pardon and love to pardon, so pardon me.

When does Ramadan 2027 begin?

In India Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 9 February; the exact date depends on the moon sighting.
