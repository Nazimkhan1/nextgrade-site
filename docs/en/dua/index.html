🤲 Duas with transliteration and meaning

The everyday duas you need most: Arabic text, transliteration in English letters, a plain English meaning and the reference. Quran text from the authenticated Tanzil text, unchanged.

  • 🌙 Roza Duas — Sehri Niyat and Iftar Dua4 duas · Intention (niyat) for the fast at Sehri · Iftar dua (the most widely recited) · Dua after breaking the fast (Dhahaba-z-zama’u) · Dua for Laylatul Qadr (Shab-e-Qadr)
  • 🍽️ Dua Before and After Eating4 duas · Before eating · If you forgot Bismillah at the start · After eating · Another dua after eating
  • 🛏️ Dua Before Sleeping and After Waking Up2 duas · Before sleeping · On waking up
  • 🚗 Travel Dua and Dua for Leaving Home2 duas · When leaving home · On mounting a vehicle / starting a journey
  • 📖 Dua for Studies and Knowledge (Rabbi Zidni Ilma)2 duas · Dua for more knowledge · The dua of Prophet Musa (AS) — before an exam, interview or speech
  • 🤲 Dua for Parents2 duas · Dua for mercy on parents · Dua for forgiveness of parents and all believers
  • ✨ Ayatul Kursi — Transliteration and Meaning1 dua · Ayatul Kursi (Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 255)
  • 📗 Rabbana Duas from the Quran3 duas · Good in this world and the next (Rabbana atina) · Forgiveness for forgetting and mistakes · For spouse, family and children

Dua means asking Allah — and Islam calls it the essence of worship. From waking up to going to sleep, for eating, travel, study and fasting, short duas have been taught for every moment so that the whole day passes in the remembrance of Allah.

Every dua in this collection has four parts: the Arabic text, the transliteration in English letters, a plain English meaning and its reference (a Quran verse or a hadith collection). Quran verses are taken word for word from the authenticated Tanzil text; they were not typed by hand.

The transliteration is a reading aid — some Arabic sounds (ع, ح, ق, خ, ض) cannot be written exactly in English letters. When teaching children, confirm the pronunciation by listening to a qari or an elder. Use the “transliteration” and “meaning” switches to show only the Arabic, and “bigger text” to make reading easier for elders.

How to make dua — the simple way

  • Begin by praising Allah (e.g. “Alhamdulillah”) and sending durood on the Prophet ﷺ.
  • Raise your hands and ask with full conviction and attention — a dua from the heart is the most precious.
  • If you do not know Arabic, you may ask in your own language — Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, English; Allah understands every language.
  • Do not be hasty or say “my dua was not answered”; be patient and keep asking.
  • Pray for your parents, family and the whole ummah along with yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make dua in English, Hindi or Urdu?

Yes — outside of salah, making dua in your own language is perfectly fine.

Is raising the hands required?

Not required, but raising the hands is Sunnah and good manners.

Which are the easiest daily duas?

“Bismillah” before eating, “Alhamdulillah” after, and the sleeping dua “Allahumma bismika amutu wa ahya”.
