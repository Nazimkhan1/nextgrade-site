Indian festivals

Every festival has its own page — why it is celebrated, how, the food that is made, and wishes to send to your loved ones. The list starts with the festivals coming up next.

🙏

Guru Nanak Jayanti · गुरु नानक जयंती

Kartik Purnima (usually November); also called Gurpurab or Prakash Parv

 🌅

Chhath Puja · छठ पूजा

Kartik Shukla Chaturthi to Saptami, six days after Diwali (13–16 November in 2026)

 🎄

Christmas · क्रिसमस

25 December every year

 🎆

New Year · नया साल

1 January (celebrations begin on the night of 31 December)

 🔥

Lohri · लोहड़ी

Usually 13 January (the evening before Makar Sankranti)

 🪁

Makar Sankranti · मकर संक्रांति

Usually 14 January (15 January in some years)

 🇮🇳

Republic Day · गणतंत्र दिवस

26 January every year

 🌼

Vasant Panchami · वसंत पंचमी

Panchami of the bright half of Magh (usually late January or February)

 🔱

Maha Shivratri · महाशिवरात्रि

Chaturdashi of the dark half of Phalgun (usually February or March)

 🎨

Holi · होली

Holika Dahan on Phalgun Purnima, the festival of colours the next day (usually March)

 🌙

Eid ul-Fitr · ईद-उल-फ़ित्र

1 Shawwal — after Ramadan, when the new moon is sighted (the calendar date moves about 10-11 days earlier each year)

 🏹

Ram Navami · राम नवमी

Navami of the bright half of Chaitra (usually March or April)

 🪢

Raksha Bandhan · रक्षा बंधन

Shravan Purnima (usually August)

 🪔

Diwali · दिवाली

Kartik Amavasya (usually October or November); five days from Dhanteras to Bhai Dooj

Festivals that follow the lunar calendar (tithi) fall on a different date every year — check the exact muhurat in your local panchang.
