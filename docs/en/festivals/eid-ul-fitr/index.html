🌙 Eid ul-Fitr · ईद-उल-फ़ित्र

Eid ul-Fitr is the festival of joy that comes after a month of fasting in Ramadan. It is also called "Meethi Eid" (sweet Eid) because every home makes sewaiyan and sweets.

When1 Shawwal — after Ramadan, when the new moon is sighted (the calendar date moves about 10-11 days earlier each year)
WhereMuslim communities all over the world; everywhere in India

Ramadan and Eid

In Ramadan, the ninth month of the Islamic (Hijri) calendar, Muslims fast from dawn to sunset, pray more and help those in need. When Ramadan ends and the Shawwal moon is sighted, Eid is celebrated the next day.

The Hijri calendar follows the moon, so the calendar date of Eid moves about 10-11 days earlier every year.

The day of Eid

In the morning people bathe, wear new clothes, eat something sweet and offer the Eid prayer at the Eidgah or mosque. Before the prayer it is necessary to give "fitra" (Zakat al-Fitr) so that poorer families can celebrate Eid too.

After the prayer people hug and say "Eid Mubarak". Elders give children "Eidi", and friends and relatives visit each other.

Food

Sheer khurma, kimami sewaiyan, milk sewaiyan, biryani and kebabs are the pride of the Eid feast.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Eid ul-Fitr and Eid ul-Adha?

Eid ul-Fitr is celebrated after Ramadan (the sweet Eid). Eid ul-Adha (Bakrid) comes about two months later, in Dhul Hijjah, at the time of Hajj.

What is fitra?

A charity given to the poor on behalf of every Muslim who can afford it, before the Eid prayer, so that they can celebrate Eid too.
