🪔 Diwali · दिवाली

Diwali (Deepavali) is the festival of lights — the victory of light over darkness and good over evil. Every home lights diyas, and Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha are worshipped.

WhenKartik Amavasya (usually October or November); five days from Dhanteras to Bhai Dooj
WhereAll over India and among Indians around the world

Why Diwali is celebrated

It is believed that after defeating Ravana and spending fourteen years in exile, Shri Ram, Mata Sita and Lakshman returned to Ayodhya on this day. The people of Ayodhya welcomed them by lighting diyas.

For Jains it is the day of Lord Mahavira's nirvana, and Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas at the same time.

Five days of festivities

Dhanteras (a day to buy utensils, gold and silver), Naraka Chaturdashi or Chhoti Diwali, the main Diwali (Lakshmi-Ganesh puja), Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj together make up the festival of lights.

Preparations and food

Before Diwali homes are cleaned and decorated and rangoli is made. Laddoos, kaju katli, shakkarpare, chakli and gujiya are prepared and sweets are shared with relatives.

Burst fewer firecrackers and follow your city's rules — the air stays cleaner and animals are not frightened.

Frequently asked questions

What are the five days of Diwali?

Dhanteras, Naraka Chaturdashi (Chhoti Diwali), Diwali (Lakshmi Puja), Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj.

Who is worshipped on Diwali?

Mainly Goddess Lakshmi (goddess of wealth and prosperity) and Lord Ganesha (god of auspicious beginnings).
