💨 Kapalbhati — follow the count

In Kapalbhati the out-breath is forceful and the in-breath happens by itself. The ball shrinks and taps with each stroke — keep to that rhythm.

How to do it

  1. Sit upright, palms on the knees.
  2. Take one normal deep breath.
  3. Snap the belly in — the breath leaves the nose quickly by itself.
  4. Let the belly relax — the breath comes in on its own. Do not breathe in hard.
  5. 20–30 strokes in a round, then rest for half a minute with normal breathing.
  6. 2–3 rounds are enough.
How long: Begin with 1–3 minutes (2–3 rounds). Build up slowly.

What you may feel

  • You feel the belly muscles working
  • The body feels light and fresh afterwards

Common mistakes

  • Breathing in hard too (only the out-breath is forceful)
  • Moving the shoulders or chest — the movement is only from the belly
  • Doing it on a full stomach
⚠️ Precautions
  • Do not do it during pregnancy or periods
  • High blood pressure, heart disease, hernia, recent abdominal or eye surgery, epilepsy — not without asking a doctor
  • Stop at once if you feel dizzy, chest pain or unease

☀️ The 10-minute morning routine

  1. ☀️Surya Namaskar — 3 rounds~4 min
  2. 👃Anulom-Vilom3 min
  3. 🐝Bhramari2 min
  4. 🌬️Deep breathing — finish calm1 min

Do it at the same time every day — every page counts your 🔥 streak.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I do Kapalbhati?

2–3 rounds of 20–30 strokes each, with rest in between, are enough to start.

When should I do it?

In the morning on an empty stomach. Never right after a meal.

Who should not do Kapalbhati?

Pregnant women and people with high BP, heart disease, hernia or a recent operation — not without medical advice.
