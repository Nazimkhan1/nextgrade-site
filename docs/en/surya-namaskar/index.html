What is Surya Namaskar?

Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation) is a sequence of 12 poses linked with the breath. Doing it once leading with the right leg and once with the left makes one full round. In India people do it in the morning on the roof or in the courtyard, facing the rising sun. Regular practice builds flexibility and gives the day a fresh start.

The 12 poses, one by one

  1. Prayer pose — प्रणामासन (Pranamasana)
    Stand straight, feet together. Join your palms in front of your chest.
    Mantra: ॐ मित्राय नमः (Om Mitraya Namah) · Breath: Breathe out
  2. Raised arms — हस्तउत्तानासन (Hasta Uttanasana)
    Breathing in, lift both arms up and lean back a little.
    Mantra: ॐ रवये नमः (Om Ravaye Namah) · Breath: Breathe in
  3. Standing forward bend — हस्तपादासन (Hastapadasana)
    Breathing out, bend forward from the hips and place your palms beside your feet. Bend the knees a little if you need to.
    Mantra: ॐ सूर्याय नमः (Om Suryaya Namah) · Breath: Breathe out
  4. Equestrian pose — अश्व संचालनासन (Ashwa Sanchalanasana)
    Breathing in, take the right leg back with the knee on the floor. Left knee between the hands, look up.
    Mantra: ॐ भानवे नमः (Om Bhanave Namah) · Breath: Breathe in
  5. Plank (stick pose) — दंडासन (Dandasana)
    Take the left leg back too. Your body is one straight line from head to heels.
    Mantra: ॐ खगाय नमः (Om Khagaya Namah) · Breath: Hold
  6. Salute with eight points — अष्टांग नमस्कार (Ashtanga Namaskara)
    Breathing out, lower knees, chest and chin to the floor; keep the hips slightly up.
    Mantra: ॐ पूष्णे नमः (Om Pushne Namah) · Breath: Breathe out
  7. Cobra pose — भुजंगासन (Bhujangasana)
    Breathing in, slide the chest forward and up, elbows slightly bent, shoulders away from the ears.
    Mantra: ॐ हिरण्यगर्भाय नमः (Om Hiranyagarbhaya Namah) · Breath: Breathe in
  8. Mountain pose — पर्वतासन (Parvatasana)
    Breathing out, lift the hips — your body forms an upside-down "V". Heels towards the floor.
    Mantra: ॐ मरीचये नमः (Om Marichaye Namah) · Breath: Breathe out
  9. Equestrian pose (other side) — अश्व संचालनासन (Ashwa Sanchalanasana)
    Breathing in, bring the right foot forward between the hands, left knee down, look up.
    Mantra: ॐ आदित्याय नमः (Om Adityaya Namah) · Breath: Breathe in
  10. Forward bend again — हस्तपादासन (Hastapadasana)
    Breathing out, bring the left foot forward too and bend forward.
    Mantra: ॐ सवित्रे नमः (Om Savitre Namah) · Breath: Breathe out
  11. Raised arms again — हस्तउत्तानासन (Hasta Uttanasana)
    Breathing in, rise slowly, arms up and a little back.
    Mantra: ॐ अर्काय नमः (Om Arkaya Namah) · Breath: Breathe in
  12. Prayer pose again — प्रणामासन (Pranamasana)
    Breathing out, bring the palms together at the chest. One round done.
    Mantra: ॐ भास्कराय नमः (Om Bhaskaraya Namah) · Breath: Breathe out

How to start

  • In the first week do 3–5 rounds a day at slow speed, then slowly build up to 12.
  • Do it on an empty stomach in the morning; wait at least 3 hours after a meal.
  • Use a mat or a durrie and wear loose clothes.
  • Never force a stretch — you may bend your knees.
  • At the end, rest for 1–2 minutes lying down (shavasana).

⚠️ Who should be careful

  • In pregnancy, or after a recent operation, do not do it without asking your doctor.
  • With high blood pressure, heart disease, hernia, or back or neck problems (slip disc), ask a doctor or yoga teacher first.
  • Stop at once if you feel dizzy, have chest pain or get very breathless.
  • Children should practise with an adult around.

Frequently asked questions

How many Surya Namaskars should I do a day?

3–5 rounds are enough to begin with. Once used to it you can do up to 12. Increase at your own pace.

When should Surya Namaskar be done?

In the morning on an empty stomach, around sunrise, is considered best. Evenings are fine too, 3 hours after a meal.

How long does one Surya Namaskar take?

About 1–1.5 minutes at a slow pace. You can choose the speed on this page.

Do I have to chant the mantras?

No. Mantras help you focus; you can also do it without them.

This is general practice information, not treatment. If you have an illness or take medicines, ask your doctor first.
