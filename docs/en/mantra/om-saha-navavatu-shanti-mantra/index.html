🕊️ Om Saha Navavatu (Shanti Mantra)

Source: Katha and Taittiriya Upanishads

ॐ सह नाववतु।

Om saha navavatu.

Om — may the Divine protect us both (teacher and student) together.

सह नौ भुनक्तु।

Saha nau bhunaktu.

May we both be nourished together.

सह वीर्यं करवावहै।

Saha viryam karavavahai.

May we work together with full energy.

तेजस्वि नावधीतमस्तु मा विद्विषावहै।

Tejasvi navadhitam astu ma vidvishavahai.

May our study be bright and fruitful; may we never resent each other.

ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः॥

Om shantih shantih shantih.

Om — peace, peace, peace: within us, with the beings around us, and from nature.

About this mantra

“Om Saha Navavatu” is a peace invocation of the Upanishads, found in the Katha and Taittiriya Upanishads. In the gurukul, teacher and student said it together before a lesson, and many schools, yoga classes and Veda pathshalas still begin with it.

Its beauty is the word “together” — “saha” and “nau” (we two) recur: learning is shared work. May both be protected, both be nourished, both work hard, and may no ill feeling ever come between them.

“Shantih” is said three times at the end. Tradition reads it as a prayer against three kinds of disturbance: from within our own body and mind, from other beings, and from nature.

Pronunciation: “navavatu” is “nau + avatu” (protect us both), so say na-va-va-tu clearly; “karavavahai” as ka-ra-va-va-hai; read “navadhitam astu” as nau-adhitam-astu; and keep the “dvi” and “sha” in “vidvishavahai” clear. Release a soft breath on the visarga of each “shantih” — the three are said slowly, each a little softer, as the peace settles. Said together at the start of a class, the mantra sets the tone for respectful, focused learning on both sides.

How to chant — the simple way

  • Sit upright in a quiet place, eyes closed or half-open, and take a few deep breaths.
  • Say the mantra slowly and clearly — aloud, in a whisper or silently; all three are accepted.
  • On a mala, one chant per bead; after 108 beads don’t cross the sumeru (big bead) — turn the mala and continue. No mala? Use the counter above.
  • The meaning matters more than the number — that is why every line has its meaning below it.

Frequently asked questions

Which Upanishad has Om Saha Navavatu?

The Katha and Taittiriya Upanishads.

Why is “shantih” said three times?

For peace from three kinds of disturbance — within us, from other beings and from nature.

When is it chanted?

Before a class, study or yoga session, by teacher and students together.

Can it be chanted before yoga?

Yes — many yoga classes open with this peace mantra, teacher and students together.
