🙏 Navkar (Namokar) Mantra

Source: The central Jain prayer (in Prakrit)

णमो अरिहंताणं।

Namo arihantanam.

I bow to the Arihantas — those who have conquered their inner enemies (anger, greed, attachment).

णमो सिद्धाणं।

Namo siddhanam.

I bow to the Siddhas — the fully liberated souls.

णमो आयरियाणं।

Namo ayariyanam.

I bow to the Acharyas — the spiritual leaders who guide the order.

णमो उवज्झायाणं।

Namo uvajjhayanam.

I bow to the Upadhyayas — the teachers of scripture.

णमो लोए सव्व साहूणं।

Namo loe savva sahunam.

I bow to all the monks and nuns in the world.

एसो पंच णमोक्कारो, सव्व पावप्पणासणो।
मंगलाणं च सव्वेसिं, पढमं हवइ मंगलं॥

Eso pancha namokkaro, savva pavappanasano.
Mangalanam cha savvesim, padhamam havai mangalam.

These five salutations destroy all wrongdoing, and of all blessings they are the first.

About this mantra

The Namokar or Navkar Mantra is the most sacred prayer of Jainism, in the Prakrit language. It names no individual or deity — it bows to the qualities that raise any soul: the Arihantas, Siddhas, Acharyas, Upadhyayas and Sadhus, together called the Pancha Parameshthi (five supreme beings).

“Navkar” comes from its nine lines — five salutations and a four-line closing (“eso pancha namokkaro…”). It asks for nothing; it teaches bowing before goodness, which is why it is called the first of all blessings.

Jain families recite it at the start of the day, before meals, before travel and at bedtime; 108 recitations on a mala are common.

The mantra is in Prakrit, not Sanskrit — so it is “namo” with a retroflex “n” (written णमो), though many families say an ordinary “namo”; both are in use. “Ayariyanam” (acharyas), “uvajjhayanam” (upadhyayas) and “sahunam” (sadhus) are Prakrit forms — don’t convert them into Sanskrit. The last four lines (the chulika) are included in the chant in some traditions and only recited in others. Many families teach it to children as the very first prayer, often before they can read, by saying it together every night.

How to chant — the simple way

  • Sit upright in a quiet place, eyes closed or half-open, and take a few deep breaths.
  • Say the mantra slowly and clearly — aloud, in a whisper or silently; all three are accepted.
  • On a mala, one chant per bead; after 108 beads don’t cross the sumeru (big bead) — turn the mala and continue. No mala? Use the counter above.
  • The meaning matters more than the number — that is why every line has its meaning below it.

Frequently asked questions

How many lines does the Navkar mantra have?

Nine — five salutations and a four-line closing.

Who are the Pancha Parameshthi?

Arihantas, Siddhas, Acharyas, Upadhyayas and Sadhus.

What language is it in?

Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi).

Is it “namo” or “ṇamo”?

Both are in use; the original Prakrit has the retroflex ṇ (णमो), while many families say namo.
