📿 Mantras with meaning

Seven of the most recited mantras and shlokas — the text in Devanagari and English letters, the meaning of every line, and a 108-bead jaap counter on every page.

  • 🌅 Gayatri MantraRigveda 3.62.10 (sage Vishvamitra)
  • 🔱 Mahamrityunjaya MantraRigveda 7.59.12 (also in the Yajurveda)
  • 🕉️ Om Namah Shivaya (Panchakshari mantra)Krishna Yajurveda, Shri Rudram (Namakam)
  • 🐘 Vakratunda Mahakaya (Ganesh mantra)Traditional Ganesh shloka
  • 🪕 Saraswati Vandana (Ya Kundendu Tushara Hara Dhavala)Traditional shloka (Saraswati Stotram)
  • 🙏 Navkar (Namokar) MantraThe central Jain prayer (in Prakrit)
  • 🕊️ Om Saha Navavatu (Shanti Mantra)Katha and Taittiriya Upanishads

Mantras are short, powerful lines repeated to steady the mind. The ones here are thousands of years old — from the Vedas, the Upanishads and tradition — and belong to everyone. The Jain Navkar Mantra is included too.

Each text follows the common reading, and the meaning of every line is written by us in plain words so that children and adults understand what they are saying. Every page also has a jaap counter — keep tapping, and 108 completes a mala.

Mala and seat: tulsi, rudraksha or sandalwood malas are common — rudraksha for Shiva mantras, tulsi for Vishnu and Krishna. Rest the mala on the middle finger of the right hand and move the beads with the thumb; by tradition the index finger does not touch the mala. Sitting on a mat of kusha grass, wool or cloth, facing east or north, is considered good. Most important of all is a little chanting at the same time every day — regularity beats quantity. If you are just starting, even one mala a day, done with attention, is a good beginning; the counter on each mantra page keeps track for you.

How to chant — the simple way

  • Sit upright in a quiet place, eyes closed or half-open, and take a few deep breaths.
  • Say the mantra slowly and clearly — aloud, in a whisper or silently; all three are accepted.
  • On a mala, one chant per bead; after 108 beads don’t cross the sumeru (big bead) — turn the mala and continue. No mala? Use the counter above.
  • The meaning matters more than the number — that is why every line has its meaning below it.

Frequently asked questions

Why 108 chants?

Tradition treats 108 as a complete, auspicious number; a mala has 108 beads.

Can I chant silently?

Yes — aloud, softly or in the mind; all three are accepted.

Can anyone recite these mantras?

Yes, they are prayers; anyone can recite them with faith and a calm mind.
