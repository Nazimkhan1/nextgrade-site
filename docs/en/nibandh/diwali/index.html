🪔 Essay on Diwali

Diwali is the festival of lights and happiness. Below are 10 lines and a short essay for small children, and a long essay with headings for higher classes.

10 lines

  1. Diwali is the biggest and most loved festival of India.
  2. It is also called Deepawali, which means "a row of lamps".
  3. This festival comes in October or November.
  4. It is believed that Lord Rama returned to Ayodhya on this day after fourteen years of exile.
  5. People clean their homes and make rangoli.
  6. In the evening, lamps, candles and strings of lights are lit.
  7. Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha are worshipped on this day.
  8. People wear new clothes and share sweets.
  9. We should burn fewer crackers so that the air stays clean.
  10. Diwali teaches us the victory of light over darkness and good over evil.

Short essay (Classes 1 to 4)

Diwali is our most loved festival. It is called the festival of lights. This festival comes in October or November. It is believed that Lord Rama returned to Ayodhya from his exile on this day. So people clean their homes, make rangoli and light lamps in the evening. Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha are worshipped on this day. We wear new clothes and share sweets. We should burn fewer crackers so that the air stays clean. Diwali teaches us to share happiness. I like Diwali very much.

Long essay (Classes 5 to 10)

Introduction

India is a land of festivals, and among them Diwali is the biggest and brightest. It is also called Deepawali, which means "a row of lamps". This festival is a symbol of the victory of light over darkness and good over evil.

Why Diwali is celebrated

It is believed that on this day Lord Rama returned to Ayodhya with Mother Sita and his brother Lakshmana after defeating Ravana and completing fourteen years of exile. To welcome him, the people of the city lit rows of oil lamps and brightened the whole city. Since then Diwali is celebrated by lighting lamps. Many people also celebrate the arrival of Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, on this day.

Preparations

Preparations begin many days before Diwali. People clean, paint and decorate their homes. Markets fill up with lamps, sweets, toys and decorations. Colourful rangoli is made at the doorstep, and homes are decorated with lamps and strings of lights.

A five-day festival

Diwali is not just one day but a festival of five days — Dhanteras, Naraka Chaturdashi (Choti Diwali), the main Diwali (Lakshmi Puja), Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj. On the evening of the main Diwali, people wear new clothes, worship Goddess Lakshmi and Lord Ganesha, decorate the home with lamps and give each other sweets and gifts.

The message of Diwali

Diwali teaches us that truth and goodness always win. The festival gives a message of love, brotherhood and sharing happiness. On this day we should also share sweets and gifts with the poor and the needy and include them in our joy.

Precautions

Crackers cause air and noise pollution and sometimes accidents too. So we should burn fewer crackers or none at all, and celebrate a "green Diwali" by lighting lamps. Small children should go near crackers only with elders.

Conclusion

Diwali is a festival not only of lights but also of a clean mind and goodness. We should celebrate it with love, safety and cleanliness. Like the lamps, we too should spread light in our own lives and in the lives of others. That is why Diwali is my favourite festival.

Frequently asked questions

What should I write in an essay on Diwali?

Introduction, why Diwali is celebrated, preparations, the five-day festival, the message of Diwali, precautions and a conclusion — the headings used in the long essay above.

Why is Diwali celebrated?

It is believed that Lord Rama returned to Ayodhya on this day after fourteen years of exile and defeating Ravana, and the people welcomed him by lighting lamps.

Why is Diwali called the festival of lights?

Because on this day people decorate their homes with many lamps, candles and lights, so it is called the festival of lights or Deepawali.
