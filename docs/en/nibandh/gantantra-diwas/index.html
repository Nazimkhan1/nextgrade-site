🇮🇳 Essay on Republic Day

Republic Day is the national festival of our Constitution and democracy, celebrated every year on 26 January. Below are 10 lines and a short essay of about 150 words for small children, and a long essay of about 500 words with headings for older classes.

10 lines

  1. Republic Day is celebrated every year on 26 January.
  2. On 26 January 1950, the Constitution of India came into force and India became a republic.
  3. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, so 26 November is Constitution Day.
  4. Dr B. R. Ambedkar was the chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution.
  5. Dr Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India.
  6. A grand parade is held on Kartavya Path in New Delhi.
  7. The President takes the salute, and colourful tableaux of the states are shown.
  8. In schools the flag is hoisted, the national anthem is sung and sweets are given.
  9. The Beating Retreat ceremony on 29 January marks the end of the celebrations.
  10. This day teaches us to respect the Constitution and be good citizens.

Short essay — about 150 words (Classes 1 to 4)

Republic Day is our national festival. It is celebrated every year on 26 January. On this day in 1950, the Constitution of India came into force and India became a republic. A republic is a country where the people choose their own leaders, and the head of the country is an elected President, not a king. Dr B. R. Ambedkar played a big role in making our Constitution, and Dr Rajendra Prasad became our first President. On Republic Day a grand parade is held on Kartavya Path in New Delhi. Soldiers march, colourful tableaux roll by and school children perform. The President takes the salute. In my school we hoist the tricolour, sing the national anthem, give speeches and sing patriotic songs. At the end we get sweets. Republic Day teaches us to love our country, follow the rules and live together in peace. I feel very proud on this day.

Long essay — about 500 words (Classes 5 to 10)

Introduction

India celebrates three national festivals — Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti and Republic Day. Republic Day has a special place among them because it reminds us of the day our Constitution came into force. Every year on 26 January the whole country is coloured in the shades of the tricolour. On 26 January 2027, India will celebrate its 78th Republic Day.

History of Republic Day

India became free on 15 August 1947, but at that time the country did not have its own Constitution to run the nation. A Constituent Assembly was formed for this work. Dr B. R. Ambedkar was the chairman of its Drafting Committee and worked very hard to prepare the draft. After nearly three years of discussion, the Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949. That is why 26 November is now celebrated as Constitution Day. The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950, and India became a republic. Dr Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India.

Why 26 January?

The date 26 January was chosen with care. On 26 January 1930, our freedom fighters had declared Purna Swaraj, the pledge for complete independence. To honour the memory of that day, the Constitution was brought into force on the same date twenty years later.

Our Constitution and the Preamble

The Preamble of the Constitution says that India is a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. This means India makes its own decisions, respects all religions equally, and its people elect their own government. The Preamble promises every citizen justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. Along with rights, the Constitution also reminds us of our duties.

The parade on Kartavya Path

The biggest celebration takes place on Kartavya Path in New Delhi. The President unfurls the national flag, the national anthem is played and the President takes the salute of the parade. Groups of the Army, Navy and Air Force march in perfect step. Tableaux from different states show the rich culture of India. Brave children and folk artists take part, and the fly-past by aircraft thrills everyone. Often the leader of another country is invited as the chief guest.

Celebrations in schools and across India

In every corner of the country, in schools, offices and neighbourhoods, the flag is hoisted. In schools children sing patriotic songs, give speeches, perform plays and drills, and sweets are given at the end. Parades are also held in the state capitals. On 29 January the Beating Retreat ceremony takes place, where military bands play sweet tunes, and it marks the end of the Republic Day celebrations.

Conclusion

Republic Day is not only a day to watch a parade. It reminds us that the real strength of a nation is its people. We should respect the Constitution, follow the rules, vote when we grow up and treat every person as an equal. If all of us do our duties honestly, our republic will grow even stronger.

Frequently asked questions

What should I write in an essay on Republic Day?

Introduction, history of Republic Day, why 26 January, the Constitution and the Preamble, the parade on Kartavya Path, celebrations in schools and a conclusion. For small classes, the 10 lines or the 150-word essay above are enough.

Why is Republic Day celebrated on 26 January?

Because the Constitution of India came into force on 26 January 1950. This date was chosen because Purna Swaraj (complete independence) was declared on 26 January 1930.

Which Republic Day is it in 2027?

On 26 January 2027, India will celebrate its 78th Republic Day.

What is the difference between Constitution Day and Republic Day?

The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, so 26 November is Constitution Day. It came into force on 26 January 1950, so 26 January is Republic Day.
