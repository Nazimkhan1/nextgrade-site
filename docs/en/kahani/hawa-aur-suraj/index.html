🌬️ The Wind and the Sun

One day the Wind and the Sun began to argue about which of them was stronger. The Wind boasted, "I can pull up trees and raise great waves in the sea!"

Just then they saw a traveller on the road below, wrapped in a warm shawl. The Sun said, "Let's see who can make this man take off his shawl. Whoever does it is the stronger one."

The Wind went first. It blew harder and harder — whoosh, whoosh! Dust flew, leaves flew. But the harder the Wind blew, the more tightly the traveller wrapped the shawl around himself. In the end the Wind grew tired and stopped.

Now it was the Sun's turn. It came out from behind the clouds and shone slowly and gently. The traveller began to feel warm. First he loosened his shawl, and a little later he took it off and slung it over his shoulder.

The Wind knew it had lost. The Sun smiled. "You see? What force cannot do, gentleness does easily."

🌟 Moral

Kindness and gentleness work better than force. Win people over with good behaviour, not with anger.

Questions to ask your child

  1. What did the Wind and the Sun argue about?
  2. What did the traveller do when the Wind blew hard?
  3. How did the Sun get the shawl off?
See the answers
  1. Which of them was stronger.
  2. He wrapped his shawl even more tightly.
  3. By shining gently until the traveller felt warm and took it off.

Words from the story

  • argue — to disagree with words
  • traveller — someone on a journey
  • gentleness — being soft and kind

In everyday life

What force cannot do, gentleness often does easily — with children and with grown-ups.

About Aesop's fables

Aesop's fables are named after Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece who is believed to have lived about two and a half thousand years ago. These short stories are mostly about animals, and each one ends with a clear lesson. Today they are told to children all over the world. 🦊 Aesop's fables →

Frequently asked questions

What is the moral of "The Wind and the Sun"?

Kindness and gentleness work better than force. Win people over with good behaviour, not with anger.

Where does the story "The Wind and the Sun" come from?

It comes from Aesop's fables, retold in simple words for children.
