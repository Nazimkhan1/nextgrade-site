🦴 The Scholars Who Brought a Lion to Life

Four friends lived in a village. Three of them had studied for years and learned great sciences, but they had little common sense. The fourth had read fewer books, but he was very wise.

The four set off to a far land to earn money. Passing through a forest, they found the bones of a dead lion. “Let’s test our learning,” said the first, and with a chant he joined all the bones into a skeleton.

The second covered the skeleton with flesh, skin and fur. Now a whole lion lay before them; only life was missing. “Now I will put life into it!” said the third.

“Stop!” cried the fourth in alarm. “It’s a lion. As soon as it lives, it will eat us all.” The three laughed. “You are a coward, and jealous of our learning.” “All right,” said the fourth, “but first let me climb this tree.” And he sat on a high branch.

The third chanted his spell — the lion opened its eyes, gave a mighty roar and leapt on the three scholars. Only the wise friend in the tree was saved. When the lion had gone, he went home with a heavy heart.

🌟 Moral

Knowledge needs good sense too. Before you do something, think about what will happen next.

About Panchatantra

The Panchatantra is a very old collection of Indian fables, believed to be around two thousand years old and credited to Pandit Vishnu Sharma. He is said to have told these animal stories to teach young princes good sense and wise conduct. It has five parts (tantras), which gives it its name, and its stories have travelled into many languages around the world. 🦁 Panchatantra stories →
