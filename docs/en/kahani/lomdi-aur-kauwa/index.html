🦊 The Clever Fox and the Crow

A crow found a big piece of bread. Happily she flew up and sat on a branch of a tall tree, so she could eat it in peace.

Just then a fox passed below. The smell of the bread made him hungry. “I want that bread,” he thought, “but the crow is up high and I can’t reach her.”

In a sweet voice the fox said, “Dear crow, how beautiful you are! Such shiny black feathers! I have heard that your voice is even lovelier. Won’t you sing me a song?”

The crow swelled with pride at the praise. As soon as she opened her beak to sing — “Caw!” — the piece of bread fell straight down.

The fox quickly picked it up and said, “Thank you, dear crow! Next time, don’t blindly trust someone just because they speak sweetly.” And off he went with the bread.

🌟 Moral

Be careful of people who give false praise. When someone talks very sweetly, think about what they want.

Questions to ask your child

  1. Where did the crow sit with the bread?
  2. What did the fox say to the crow?
  3. How did the bread fall?
See the answers
  1. On a branch of a tall tree.
  2. He praised her beauty and asked her to sing.
  3. She opened her beak to sing and dropped it.

Words from the story

  • praise — saying good things about someone
  • flattery — false praise to get something
  • branch — an arm of a tree

In everyday life

Don’t trust sweet words and false praise blindly. When someone praises you a lot, ask what they might want.

About Aesop's fables

Aesop's fables are named after Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece who is believed to have lived about two and a half thousand years ago. These short stories are mostly about animals, and each one ends with a clear lesson. Today they are told to children all over the world. 🦊 Aesop's fables →
