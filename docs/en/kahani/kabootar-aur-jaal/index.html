🕊️ The Pigeons and the Hunter's Net

A big flock of pigeons lived in a forest. Their king was very wise. One day, while looking for food, the flock saw rice grains scattered on the ground. Everyone was hungry, so they flew down without thinking.

As soon as they began pecking, a net dropped from above and all the pigeons were trapped. The hunter hiding in a far-off bush started walking towards them happily. The frightened pigeons flapped about in every direction.

Then the king of the pigeons said, "Don't panic! If we fly in different directions, we'll only get more tangled. Let's all push together, the same way — on my count: one, two, three!"

All the pigeons flew up together — and the whole net rose into the air with them! The hunter could only stare. The pigeons flew far away to a hill where the king's friend, a mouse, lived.

The mouse began cutting the net with his teeth. The king said, "Free my companions first, and me last of all." The mouse freed them one by one. Together they thanked the mouse and flew home happily.

🌟 Moral

There is great strength in unity. And a good leader thinks of his companions first.

Questions to ask your child

  1. How were the pigeons trapped?
  2. What did the pigeon king tell them to do?
  3. What did the king ask the mouse?
See the answers
  1. They saw rice on the ground and flew down without thinking.
  2. All fly up together, in the same direction.
  3. To free his companions first and him last.

Words from the story

  • flock — a group of birds
  • unity — working together as one
  • hunter — someone who catches animals

In everyday life

When we pull together in one direction, even a heavy problem lifts. A good leader thinks of others first.

About Panchatantra

The Panchatantra is a very old collection of Indian fables, believed to be around two thousand years old and credited to Pandit Vishnu Sharma. He is said to have told these animal stories to teach young princes good sense and wise conduct. It has five parts (tantras), which gives it its name, and its stories have travelled into many languages around the world. 🦁 Panchatantra stories →
