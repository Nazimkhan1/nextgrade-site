🦌 The Golden Deer

In a thick forest lived a golden deer named Ruru. His coat shone like gold, and he was very kind. One day he heard the cries of a man drowning in the river.

Without fear, Ruru leapt into the fast current, put the man on his back and brought him to the bank. The man folded his hands. “You saved my life. Tell me, what can I do for you?” Ruru said, “Just promise not to tell anyone about me, or people will come to catch me.” The man promised.

Some days later the queen of that kingdom saw a golden deer in a dream and begged the king to bring it to her. The king announced, “Whoever tells me where the golden deer lives will be rewarded with a village.” Tempted by greed, the same man forgot his promise and told the king where Ruru lived.

The king came to the forest with his soldiers and aimed an arrow at Ruru. Calmly Ruru asked, “King, who told you where to find me?” The king pointed at the man. Then Ruru told the whole story of how he had saved that very man’s life.

The king was deeply saddened to hear it. He lowered his bow, scolded the ungrateful man and sent him away. Then he ordered that no one in his kingdom would ever hunt the animals of that forest. Ruru and all the animals lived without fear again.

🌟 Moral

Never forget a kindness done to you, and don’t break a promise out of greed. Kindness is rewarded in the end.

About Jataka tales

Jataka tales come from the Buddhist tradition and tell of the earlier lives of the Buddha. In them the Bodhisattva appears as an animal, a king or an ordinary person, and shows the way of kindness, honesty and wisdom. These stories have been told for thousands of years. 🪷 Jataka tales →

It comes from the Jataka tales, retold in simple words for children.
