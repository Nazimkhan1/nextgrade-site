🦀 The Crane and the Crab

An old crane lived beside a pond. He could no longer catch fish as quickly as before, and he often went hungry. One day he thought of a trick.

He sat sadly on the bank. The fish asked, "What's wrong, Grandpa Crane?" The crane said, "Terrible news. There will be no rain this year, and this pond will dry up. But beyond the hill there is a big lake — I will carry you there one by one."

The frightened fish agreed. Every day the crane flew off holding one fish in his beak — but not to the lake. He took it to a rock nearby and ate it. A pile of bones grew on the rock.

A crab also lived in the pond. He said, "Grandpa Crane, take me today." The crane was pleased and flew off with the crab sitting on his neck. From the sky the crab saw the bones on the rock and understood everything.

The crab gripped the crane's neck tightly with his strong claws and said, "Take me back to the pond, or I won't let go!" The frightened crane had to turn back. The crab told all the fish the truth, and the cheating crane was never seen at that pond again.

🌟 Moral

Don't trust someone blindly just because their words are sweet. Even when you are scared, think before you decide — and face the truth with courage.

Questions to ask your child

  1. Where did the crane say he would take the fish?
  2. What did he really do with them?
  3. How did the crab learn the truth?
See the answers
  1. To a big lake beyond the hill.
  2. He took them to a rock nearby and ate them.
  3. From the sky he saw the pile of bones on the rock.

Words from the story

  • crane — a tall bird with long legs that eats fish
  • claws — the strong pincers of a crab
  • trick — a clever plan to fool someone

In everyday life

If someone frightens you and then offers “help”, don’t trust them blindly — check first. Stay alert like the crab.

About Panchatantra

The Panchatantra is a very old collection of Indian fables, believed to be around two thousand years old and credited to Pandit Vishnu Sharma. He is said to have told these animal stories to teach young princes good sense and wise conduct. It has five parts (tantras), which gives it its name, and its stories have travelled into many languages around the world. 🦁 Panchatantra stories →
