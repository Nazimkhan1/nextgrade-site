🐴 Tenali Raman’s Horse

To build up his cavalry, King Krishnadevaraya gave each courtier a horse, along with money every month for its feed. The condition was that after three months the horses must look healthy.

Tenali Raman tied his horse in a small shed with only one tiny window in the wall. Every day he pushed a little grass in through that window. With the horse’s money he ran his own household.

After three months everyone came with their horses. Tenali came empty-handed. “Your Majesty,” he said, “my horse has become so strong and fierce that I can’t bring him. Send someone to see for himself.” The king sent the officer in charge of his horses, a man with a long, thick beard.

As soon as the officer peeped through the window, the hungry horse thought his beard was grass and grabbed it in its teeth! The officer yelled, but the horse would not let go. People had to break down the shed wall to free him.

The king saw that the horse was thin as a stick! He asked Tenali why. Tenali smiled. “Your Majesty, you have seen how strong a horse is when it is hungry enough to grab a beard. But the truth is, I was wrong — the horse’s share should have gone to the horse.” The king laughed, but he ordered Tenali to feed the horse properly for the next three months.

🌟 Moral

What is given for someone else should go to them. Cleverness may save you for a while, but honesty is the right path.

Questions to ask your child

  1. What did the king give along with each horse?
  2. Where did Tenali keep his horse?
  3. Why did the horse grab the officer’s beard?
See the answers
  1. Money every month for its feed.
  2. In a small shed with only one tiny window.
  3. It was so hungry it thought the beard was grass.

Words from the story

  • feed — food for animals
  • cavalry — soldiers on horses
  • shed — a small simple hut

In everyday life

Using what is meant for someone else is wrong — and Tenali admitted his mistake. Owning up to a mistake takes courage too.

About Tenali Raman

Tenali Raman (Tenali Ramakrishna) was a poet in the court of King Krishnadevaraya of Vijayanagara. He is remembered for his cleverness and sense of humour. His stories began in South India and are now told to children all over the country. 🧠 Tenali Raman stories →
