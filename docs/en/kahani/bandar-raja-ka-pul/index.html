🐒 The Monkey King’s Bridge

Long, long ago a huge mango tree grew on the bank of the Ganga. Eighty thousand monkeys lived in it, and their king was a big, strong and kind monkey. He always said, “Never let a mango fall into the river, or humans will come here.”

But one day a mango hidden among the leaves fell into the river and floated all the way to a king’s palace. The king tasted it — he had never eaten such a sweet mango. He set out in boats with his soldiers to find the tree.

At night they found it. Seeing the monkeys eating mangoes, the king told his soldiers, “Surround them in the morning.” The monkeys were terrified. The monkey king thought, “It is my duty to save my people.”

Holding a long vine, he leapt over the river to a tree on the other side. He tied one end there and the other around his waist. But the vine was a little too short, so he gripped a branch of the mango tree with his hands and made his own body into a bridge. One by one, all the monkeys walked across his back to the other side.

The human king had watched everything, and his eyes filled with tears. “Let no one harm him,” he told his soldiers. He had the tired monkey king gently brought down and cared for. The monkey king said, “A true king puts the happiness of his people above his own.” The human king remembered those words all his life.

🌟 Moral

A true leader is one who suffers hardship to protect others.

About Jataka tales

Jataka tales come from the Buddhist tradition and tell of the earlier lives of the Buddha. In them the Bodhisattva appears as an animal, a king or an ordinary person, and shows the way of kindness, honesty and wisdom. These stories have been told for thousands of years. 🪷 Jataka tales →
