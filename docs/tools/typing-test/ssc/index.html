SSC टाइपिंग टेस्ट अभ्यास — 10 मिनट का इंग्लिश टेस्ट

10 मिनट का इंग्लिश टाइपिंग टेस्ट, बैकस्पेस चालू — सरकारी भर्ती के स्किल टेस्ट जैसा अभ्यास। हिंदी में देना हो तो ऊपर भाषा बदल लें।

🔒 आप जो टाइप करते हैं वह कहीं नहीं जाता। परिणाम सिर्फ़ इसी ब्राउज़र में सेव होते हैं।

किस भाषा में टाइप करना है?

कितने मिनट?

बैकस्पेस

Nukta

⌨️

📱 फ़ोन पर भी चलता है, लेकिन परीक्षा कंप्यूटर कीबोर्ड पर होती है — अभ्यास भी कीबोर्ड पर करें तो बेहतर।

Only a few years ago, most people in India carried cash for almost every purchase. Today, a vegetable seller, a tea stall and a large shop may all accept payment through a mobile phone. The customer simply scans a printed code, enters the amount and confirms the payment with a secret number. Within seconds, the money moves from one bank account to another. Digital payments have brought many benefits. People no longer need to worry about carrying the exact change or about losing a fat wallet on a crowded bus. Small traders can keep a clear record of their daily sales, which helps them when they apply for a loan. Families living far apart can send money to each other at any hour, even on holidays. Bills for electricity, water, gas and mobile phones can be paid from home without standing in long queues. However, the new system also asks for new care. Fraudsters often try to trick people with fake calls and messages. They may pretend to be bank officers, delivery agents or even relatives in trouble. The most important rule is simple: never share your secret number or the one-time password with anyone, no matter who they claim to be. Remember that you never need to enter your secret number to receive money. It is also wise to check the name shown on the screen before confirming a payment and to keep the app updated. If something goes wrong, the bank should be informed at once, because quick action improves the chance of getting the money back. Cash has not disappeared, and many older people still prefer it. Both systems will probably exist side by side for a long time. What matters is that every user understands how to pay safely and teaches the same to others in the family. Farmers are often called the backbone of the country, and for good reason. The grain in our kitchens, the vegetables in the market and the milk on our table all depend on their hard work. Yet most of us know very little about the life of a farmer and the many risks he or she faces every season. A farmer's day begins before sunrise. There are animals to feed, fields to water and crops to watch over. The work changes with the season. At sowing time, the soil has to be ploughed and the seeds chosen with care. As the plants grow, they must be protected from weeds, insects and diseases. At harvest time, the whole family often joins in, and the work goes on from morning to night. Farming depends heavily on the weather. Too little rain can dry up the crop, while too much rain or a sudden hailstorm can destroy months of effort in a single afternoon. Prices in the market also go up and down, and a good harvest does not always mean a good income. Many small farmers own only a little land and have to borrow money to buy seeds, fertiliser and diesel. In recent years, new ideas have started to help. Drip irrigation saves water by delivering it straight to the roots. Soil testing tells the farmer which nutrients the land really needs, so that fertiliser is not wasted. Mobile phones bring weather alerts and market prices to the village. Farmers who form groups can buy inputs together and sell their produce at better rates. Respecting the farmer is not only a matter of words. We can avoid wasting food, buy directly from local growers when possible and support efforts that make farming a safer and more rewarding way of life. Few events are awaited in India as eagerly as the arrival of the monsoon. After weeks of scorching heat, when the ground is cracked and the air feels heavy, the first dark clouds bring hope to millions of people. Children run outside to feel the first drops, and the smell of wet earth spreads through every lane. The monsoon is far more than a pleasant change in the weather. A large share of the country's yearly rainfall comes during these few months. The rain fills ponds, lakes and wells, raises the level of water under the ground and keeps the rivers flowing. Farmers plan their sowing around its arrival, and a good monsoon usually means a good harvest and steady prices in the market. But the rains also bring difficulties. In cities, poor drainage causes water to collect on roads, and traffic can stop for hours. In low lying areas, rivers may overflow and enter homes and fields. Landslides can block roads in the hills. Stagnant water becomes a breeding ground for mosquitoes, so diseases such as dengue and malaria tend to rise during this season. A little preparation can reduce many of these problems. Drains should be cleaned before the rains begin, and rooftops can be fitted to collect rainwater in tanks. Families should keep a torch, some dry food and important papers in a safe, waterproof place. Water stored in coolers, pots and old tyres should be emptied regularly so that mosquitoes cannot breed. The weather department releases forecasts and warnings throughout the season, and it is wise to follow them. The monsoon is both a blessing and a test. If we respect it and plan carefully, we can enjoy its gifts and keep its dangers under control. Sports are often treated as a break from studies, but in truth they are an important part of education. A child who plays regularly grows stronger, sleeps better and finds it easier to concentrate in class. Games also teach lessons that no textbook can teach in quite the same way. On the playground, children learn to follow rules, to wait for their turn and to accept the decision of the referee. They learn that practice matters more than luck, and that losing a match is not the end of the world. A team game such as football, hockey or kabaddi shows that the group can achieve much more when every member plays his or her part. Individual sports such as running, swimming or badminton build self discipline, because the player has to push on even when nobody is watching. India has a rich tradition of games. Many villages still play kabaddi, kho kho and wrestling, which need very little equipment and can be enjoyed by anyone. At the same time, more young people are now taking up shooting, boxing, archery and athletics, and some of them have won medals at the highest level. Their success has inspired many families to look at sports as a possible career. Yet many schools still do not have a proper playground or a trained coach. In some homes, girls are discouraged from playing outside after a certain age. These problems can be solved with will and planning. Empty public land can be turned into playgrounds, and local clubs can organise regular tournaments. Parents can help by giving children time to play every day and by cheering for effort, not only for results. A nation that plays together stays healthy, confident and united. Science is not something that lives only inside laboratories and textbooks. From the moment we wake up until we go to sleep, we use its results in countless ways, often without noticing them at all. Think about an ordinary morning. The alarm on a mobile phone works because of electronic circuits and a tiny battery. The pressure cooker in the kitchen cooks food faster because steam trapped inside raises the boiling point of water. Soap removes dirt because its molecules can hold on to both oil and water at the same time. Even the simple act of boiling water before drinking it is based on the knowledge that heat kills harmful germs. Science also helps us stay healthy. Vaccines train the body to fight diseases before they strike. Doctors use X rays and other scanning machines to look inside the body without cutting it open. Clean drinking water, proper sanitation and refrigerated medicines have saved more lives than almost any single invention. Travel and communication have changed in the same way. Satellites help us find our route on a map, forecast the weather and carry television signals across the country. A message typed in one corner of India can reach another corner in less than a second. However, learning facts is not enough. The real spirit of science lies in asking questions, testing ideas and accepting the answer that the evidence supports. This attitude protects us from rumours and false claims that spread so easily today. Before believing a strange message or a miracle cure, we should ask where it came from and whether it has been tested. Students who develop this habit of careful thinking will be better citizens, whatever career they choose. Science, after all, is simply a way of understanding the world honestly.

स्किल टेस्ट में अक्सर क्या होता है?

बहुत सी सरकारी भर्तियों में, जैसे क्लर्क या डेटा एंट्री वाले पदों में, लिखित परीक्षा पास करने के बाद एक टाइपिंग स्किल टेस्ट होता है। अक्सर यह कंप्यूटर पर होता है: स्क्रीन पर एक अनुच्छेद दिखता है और नीचे एक बॉक्स में उसे टाइप करना होता है, जैसा इस पेज पर है। समय अक्सर 10 या 15 मिनट के आसपास होता है, और कई भर्तियों में यह टेस्ट सिर्फ़ क्वालिफ़ाइंग होता है — यानी पास होना ज़रूरी है, लेकिन इसके अंक अंतिम मेरिट में नहीं जुड़ते। किस भर्ती में कितना समय, कितनी स्पीड और कितनी ग़लती की छूट है, यह हर बार अधिसूचना में लिखा होता है; वही मानें।

स्पीड की सीमा और ग़लतियाँ

इंग्लिश के लिए अक्सर 35 WPM के आसपास और हिंदी के लिए 30 WPM के आसपास जैसी सीमाएँ सुनने को मिलती हैं, लेकिन इसे पक्का न मानें — हर भर्ती अपनी सीमा ख़ुद बताती है। कई जगह स्पीड कीस्ट्रोक से नापी जाती है, और ग़लतियों की एक तय प्रतिशत तक छूट होती है। कुछ ग़लतियाँ पूरी और कुछ आधी गिनी जा सकती हैं, जैसे स्पेसिंग या विराम चिह्न। यह टूल हर ग़लत, छूटे या फ़ालतू शब्द को पूरी ग़लती गिनता है, इसलिए यहाँ का नेट WPM थोड़ा सख़्त अंदाज़ा है — यह अच्छी बात है।

10 मिनट के टेस्ट की तैयारी

  1. पहले 5 मिनट के टेस्ट में लगातार अपनी लक्ष्य स्पीड पार करें।
  2. फिर 10 मिनट के टेस्ट दें और देखें कि आख़िरी मिनटों में स्पीड कितनी गिरती है।
  3. अगर शुद्धता 92–93% से नीचे जाए तो स्पीड कम करें; ग़लतियाँ अक्सर ज़्यादा नुक़सान करती हैं।
  4. हफ़्ते में दो बार बैकस्पेस बंद करके भी दें, ताकि सुधारने में समय बर्बाद करने की आदत छूटे।
  5. परीक्षा से पहले के हफ़्ते रोज़ एक ही समय पर टेस्ट दें, जैसे असली परीक्षा का समय हो।

परीक्षा के दिन के लिए काम की बातें

  • शुरू होने से पहले कुर्सी, कीबोर्ड और स्क्रीन की दूरी ठीक कर लें।
  • पहली पंक्ति पढ़कर शुरू करें; घबराहट में शुरुआत की ग़लतियाँ सबसे आम हैं।
  • एक शब्द ग़लत हो जाए तो आगे बढ़ें — कई बार पीछे जाकर सुधारने में ज़्यादा समय जाता है।
  • पंक्ति बदलते समय उँगली की जगह आँख से याद रखें कि कौन सा शब्द था।
  • कीबोर्ड नया या अलग हो सकता है; इसलिए अभ्यास में भी कभी-कभी दूसरे कीबोर्ड पर टाइप करें।

इस पेज का टूल

यहाँ 10 मिनट और इंग्लिश पहले से चुने हुए हैं, और बैकस्पेस चालू है। आप चाहें तो समय, भाषा या बैकस्पेस बदल सकते हैं। हर टेस्ट का परिणाम आपके ब्राउज़र में सेव होता है — किसी सर्वर पर नहीं — ताकि आप हफ़्ते-दर-हफ़्ते अपनी प्रगति देख सकें।

अक्सर पूछे जाने वाले सवाल

क्या यह असली SSC टेस्ट है?

नहीं। यह एक फ़्री अभ्यास टूल है जो स्किल टेस्ट जैसा माहौल देता है। असली टेस्ट का तरीक़ा, समय और नियम सिर्फ़ आधिकारिक अधिसूचना में होते हैं।

SSC जैसे टेस्ट में स्पीड कैसे नापी जाती है?

अक्सर कीस्ट्रोक के हिसाब से — जैसे 5 कीस्ट्रोक को एक शब्द माना जाता है — और ग़लतियों की एक सीमा तय होती है। सही तरीक़ा हर अधिसूचना में देखें।

हिंदी में स्किल टेस्ट का अभ्यास कैसे करें?

ऊपर भाषा में हिंदी चुन लें और 10 मिनट रखें। हिंदी के लिए डिवाइस में यूनिकोड हिंदी कीबोर्ड चाहिए।

10 मिनट में कितने शब्द टाइप करने होते हैं?

यह स्पीड पर निर्भर है: 35 WPM की स्पीड पर 10 मिनट में लगभग 1750 कीस्ट्रोक, यानी क़रीब 350 "शब्द" बनते हैं।

बैकस्पेस परीक्षा में चलता है?

यह हर टेस्ट के सॉफ़्टवेयर और नियम पर निर्भर है। इसलिए दोनों तरह अभ्यास करना अच्छा है — इस पेज पर बैकस्पेस चालू है, ऊपर से बंद कर सकते हैं।
