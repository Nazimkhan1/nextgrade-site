Every year, after the harvest is over, a large fair is held in the open ground near our village. For weeks before it begins, people talk of little else. Shopkeepers from nearby towns arrive with carts and trucks, and within a few days an empty field turns into a bright and busy market. On the first morning, the whole village seems to be on the move. Grandparents walk slowly with their walking sticks, young couples carry their babies, and children run ahead, unable to wait. The air is filled with the sound of drums, the call of sellers and the music from loudspeakers. There is something for everyone at the fair. Women gather around stalls selling bangles, cloth and kitchen utensils. Farmers inspect new tools, seeds and water pumps, and some of them come only to buy or sell cattle. Children spend their pocket money on balloons, whistles and clay toys. The sweet shops do the best business of all, with hot jalebis being fried in huge pans from morning till late at night. In the evening, the giant wheel lights up and long queues form near the swings. A small stage is set up where local artists sing folk songs and perform short plays. Some of the stories are funny, and some carry a message about education, health or saving water. The fair is also a meeting place. Relatives who live in different villages meet here once a year and share news of weddings, births and good crops. Old friends sit together over tea and talk for hours. When the fair ends and the tents are taken down, the field looks strangely empty. Yet the memories remain, and everyone starts counting the days until the next harvest brings the fair back again. In an age of mobile screens and short videos, the habit of reading a full book is slowly becoming rare. Yet reading remains one of the best ways to grow the mind. A good book can take us to places we have never visited, introduce us to people we will never meet and help us understand feelings we cannot always express. Reading improves language skills in a natural way. A person who reads regularly picks up new words, learns how sentences are built and starts writing more clearly without special effort. This is very useful for students preparing for competitive examinations, where comprehension passages, essays and letters are a part of the test. Reading the newspaper every day also keeps us aware of what is happening around us. Public libraries have an important role to play here. Not every family can afford to buy many books, but a library allows anyone to borrow them for free or for a small fee. A quiet reading room is a blessing for students who do not have enough space to study at home. Many libraries also keep magazines, maps and old newspapers that are useful for research. Sadly, some libraries suffer from old furniture, poor lighting and a lack of new books. Local bodies, schools and readers can work together to change this. People can donate books they have already read, volunteers can run story sessions for children, and libraries can open for longer hours during examination season. Parents can begin early by reading aloud to young children and keeping a few books within their reach at home. Even fifteen minutes of reading every day can make a difference over a year. A reading nation is a thinking nation, and thinking citizens are the strongest foundation of any democracy. Water is so common in our daily life that we rarely stop to think about where it comes from. We turn on a tap and expect it to flow. But in many towns and villages, women and children still walk long distances to fetch water, and in summer even big cities face shortages. Saving water is therefore not a choice but a necessity. Most of the water we use comes from rivers, lakes and the ground beneath our feet. When rain falls, part of it soaks into the soil and fills the underground stores. In many areas, more water is being pumped out each year than the rain can put back, so the water level keeps falling. Wells that once had water throughout the year now dry up soon after the winter. At home, a lot of water is wasted through small habits. A leaking tap can waste many litres in a single day. Leaving the tap running while brushing teeth or washing dishes adds up quickly. Using a bucket instead of a hose to wash a vehicle, and a mug instead of a shower when water is short, can make a real difference. Water used for washing vegetables can be poured on plants instead of down the drain. Rainwater harvesting is one of the best long term solutions. Rain falling on rooftops can be collected in tanks or guided into pits that recharge the ground. Old village ponds, which once stored water for the whole year, can be cleaned and revived with the help of the community. Farmers can save water by choosing suitable crops and using drip or sprinkler irrigation. Industries can treat and reuse their waste water. Every drop saved today is a drop available for our children tomorrow. India receives bright sunshine for a large part of the year, and this sunshine is a source of clean energy that never runs out. For a long time, most of our electricity came from burning coal, which pollutes the air and adds to the warming of the planet. Today, solar energy is growing quickly and changing the way many homes, farms and offices get their power. A solar panel is made of special cells that turn sunlight directly into electricity. There are no moving parts, so the panels make no noise and need little care apart from regular cleaning of dust. Panels can be fixed on the roof of a house, on top of a school building or over a large stretch of dry land. The power produced can be used immediately, stored in batteries or sent to the main grid. Solar energy has made a special difference in remote villages. Places that once remained dark after sunset now have street lights, and children can study in the evening. Solar pumps help farmers water their fields without spending money on diesel. Small shops use solar lamps to stay open longer and earn more. There are still some challenges. The panels produce power only during the day, and their output drops on cloudy days and during the rainy season. Batteries are still costly and need to be replaced after some years. Old panels must be disposed of carefully so that they do not harm the environment. Even so, the benefits are clear. A family that installs a rooftop system can lower its electricity bill and reduce pollution at the same time. As the cost of panels keeps falling and more people learn to use them, the sun may become one of the most important partners in the country's progress. Rivers have shaped the life of India for thousands of years. Towns and villages grew up along their banks because the water was needed for drinking, for farming and for trade. Even today, a large part of the country depends on rivers for its daily needs. In the north, rivers fed by melting snow keep flowing through the summer, while many rivers in the south rise and fall with the rain. When the monsoon arrives, small streams turn into wide, fast rivers within a few days, and the farmers living nearby watch the water level closely. A river is much more than a line on a map. It carries soft soil from the hills and spreads it over the plains, which makes the land rich for crops. Fishermen earn their living from it, boats carry people and goods across it, and children learn to swim in its shallow parts. Many fairs and festivals are held on river banks, and people travel long distances to take part in them. However, our rivers are under great pressure. Waste from homes and factories often flows into them without proper treatment. Plastic bags and bottles collect along the banks and block the flow of water. In some places, too much sand is taken from the river bed, which weakens the banks and harms the fish. When forests near the source are cut down, the soil washes away and the river becomes muddy and shallow. Keeping rivers clean is not the job of the government alone. Every family can help by not throwing waste into the water, by using less plastic and by saving water at home. Schools can take students on visits to the river so that they understand its value. If each of us does a small part, our rivers can remain alive and healthy for the generations to come.

स्किल टेस्ट में अक्सर क्या होता है?

बहुत सी सरकारी भर्तियों में, जैसे क्लर्क या डेटा एंट्री वाले पदों में, लिखित परीक्षा पास करने के बाद एक टाइपिंग स्किल टेस्ट होता है। अक्सर यह कंप्यूटर पर होता है: स्क्रीन पर एक अनुच्छेद दिखता है और नीचे एक बॉक्स में उसे टाइप करना होता है, जैसा इस पेज पर है। समय अक्सर 10 या 15 मिनट के आसपास होता है, और कई भर्तियों में यह टेस्ट सिर्फ़ क्वालिफ़ाइंग होता है — यानी पास होना ज़रूरी है, लेकिन इसके अंक अंतिम मेरिट में नहीं जुड़ते। किस भर्ती में कितना समय, कितनी स्पीड और कितनी ग़लती की छूट है, यह हर बार अधिसूचना में लिखा होता है; वही मानें।

स्पीड की सीमा और ग़लतियाँ

इंग्लिश के लिए अक्सर 35 WPM के आसपास और हिंदी के लिए 30 WPM के आसपास जैसी सीमाएँ सुनने को मिलती हैं, लेकिन इसे पक्का न मानें — हर भर्ती अपनी सीमा ख़ुद बताती है। कई जगह स्पीड कीस्ट्रोक से नापी जाती है, और ग़लतियों की एक तय प्रतिशत तक छूट होती है। कुछ ग़लतियाँ पूरी और कुछ आधी गिनी जा सकती हैं, जैसे स्पेसिंग या विराम चिह्न। यह टूल हर ग़लत, छूटे या फ़ालतू शब्द को पूरी ग़लती गिनता है, इसलिए यहाँ का नेट WPM थोड़ा सख़्त अंदाज़ा है — यह अच्छी बात है।

10 मिनट के टेस्ट की तैयारी

  1. पहले 5 मिनट के टेस्ट में लगातार अपनी लक्ष्य स्पीड पार करें।
  2. फिर 10 मिनट के टेस्ट दें और देखें कि आख़िरी मिनटों में स्पीड कितनी गिरती है।
  3. अगर शुद्धता 92–93% से नीचे जाए तो स्पीड कम करें; ग़लतियाँ अक्सर ज़्यादा नुक़सान करती हैं।
  4. हफ़्ते में दो बार बैकस्पेस बंद करके भी दें, ताकि सुधारने में समय बर्बाद करने की आदत छूटे।
  5. परीक्षा से पहले के हफ़्ते रोज़ एक ही समय पर टेस्ट दें, जैसे असली परीक्षा का समय हो।

परीक्षा के दिन के लिए काम की बातें

  • शुरू होने से पहले कुर्सी, कीबोर्ड और स्क्रीन की दूरी ठीक कर लें।
  • पहली पंक्ति पढ़कर शुरू करें; घबराहट में शुरुआत की ग़लतियाँ सबसे आम हैं।
  • एक शब्द ग़लत हो जाए तो आगे बढ़ें — कई बार पीछे जाकर सुधारने में ज़्यादा समय जाता है।
  • पंक्ति बदलते समय उँगली की जगह आँख से याद रखें कि कौन सा शब्द था।
  • कीबोर्ड नया या अलग हो सकता है; इसलिए अभ्यास में भी कभी-कभी दूसरे कीबोर्ड पर टाइप करें।

इस पेज का टूल

यहाँ 10 मिनट और इंग्लिश पहले से चुने हुए हैं, और बैकस्पेस चालू है। आप चाहें तो समय, भाषा या बैकस्पेस बदल सकते हैं। हर टेस्ट का परिणाम आपके ब्राउज़र में सेव होता है — किसी सर्वर पर नहीं — ताकि आप हफ़्ते-दर-हफ़्ते अपनी प्रगति देख सकें।

अक्सर पूछे जाने वाले सवाल

क्या यह असली SSC टेस्ट है?

नहीं। यह एक फ़्री अभ्यास टूल है जो स्किल टेस्ट जैसा माहौल देता है। असली टेस्ट का तरीक़ा, समय और नियम सिर्फ़ आधिकारिक अधिसूचना में होते हैं।

SSC जैसे टेस्ट में स्पीड कैसे नापी जाती है?

अक्सर कीस्ट्रोक के हिसाब से — जैसे 5 कीस्ट्रोक को एक शब्द माना जाता है — और ग़लतियों की एक सीमा तय होती है। सही तरीक़ा हर अधिसूचना में देखें।

हिंदी में स्किल टेस्ट का अभ्यास कैसे करें?

ऊपर भाषा में हिंदी चुन लें और 10 मिनट रखें। हिंदी के लिए डिवाइस में यूनिकोड हिंदी कीबोर्ड चाहिए।

10 मिनट में कितने शब्द टाइप करने होते हैं?

यह स्पीड पर निर्भर है: 35 WPM की स्पीड पर 10 मिनट में लगभग 1750 कीस्ट्रोक, यानी क़रीब 350 "शब्द" बनते हैं।

बैकस्पेस परीक्षा में चलता है?

यह हर टेस्ट के सॉफ़्टवेयर और नियम पर निर्भर है। इसलिए दोनों तरह अभ्यास करना अच्छा है — इस पेज पर बैकस्पेस चालू है, ऊपर से बंद कर सकते हैं।
