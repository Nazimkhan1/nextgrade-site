For millions of people in India, the train is the most common way to travel long distances. Every day, trains carry students going to college, workers going to their jobs, families visiting relatives and traders moving their goods from one city to another. The railway station is a small world of its own, full of noise, colour and movement at almost every hour of the day and night. A long train journey teaches us a great deal about the country. As the train moves from one state to another, the language on the signboards changes, the food sold on the platforms changes and even the shape of the houses outside the window changes. Passengers who were strangers in the morning share snacks and stories by the evening. Many people still remember the first time they travelled by train with their parents. Running such a large network is not an easy task. Thousands of workers look after the tracks, the signals, the engines and the coaches. Station masters, guards, drivers and ticket checkers work in shifts so that the trains can keep running safely. Tracks have to be checked regularly, especially during heavy rain and in very cold weather, when the risk of damage is higher. In recent years, many changes have made train travel easier. Tickets can be booked on a mobile phone, the running status of a train can be checked online, and many stations now have cleaner waiting rooms, lifts and better lighting. Some new trains are faster and more comfortable than the older ones. Passengers also have a duty. They should keep the coaches clean, respect the people around them and never travel on the roof or the footboard. Railway property belongs to the whole nation, and taking care of it means taking care of ourselves. Cleanliness is a habit that begins at home but affects the whole society. A clean house, a clean street and a clean market make life healthier and more pleasant for everyone. When waste is left lying in the open, it attracts flies, mosquitoes and stray animals, and it becomes a source of many diseases. Most of these illnesses can be avoided if we follow a few simple rules every day. The first rule is to keep waste in its proper place. Every home should have separate bins for wet waste, such as vegetable peels and leftover food, and dry waste, such as paper, plastic and glass. Wet waste can be turned into compost for plants, while much of the dry waste can be sent for recycling. When waste is separated at the source, the work of the sanitation workers becomes much easier and less of it reaches the landfill. The second rule is personal hygiene. Washing hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet is one of the cheapest and most effective ways to stay healthy. Children learn such habits quickly when they see adults following them. The third rule concerns public places. Many people keep their homes spotless but do not think twice before throwing a wrapper on the road or spitting at a bus stop. Public spaces belong to all of us, and keeping them clean is a shared duty. Schools, offices and residents' groups can organise cleaning drives from time to time and plant trees in empty plots. Cleanliness campaigns have shown that change is possible when people decide to act together. A town does not become clean in a single day, but every small effort counts. If each person decides not to litter and to speak politely to those who do, our surroundings will slowly but surely improve. Only a few years ago, most people in India carried cash for almost every purchase. Today, a vegetable seller, a tea stall and a large shop may all accept payment through a mobile phone. The customer simply scans a printed code, enters the amount and confirms the payment with a secret number. Within seconds, the money moves from one bank account to another. Digital payments have brought many benefits. People no longer need to worry about carrying the exact change or about losing a fat wallet on a crowded bus. Small traders can keep a clear record of their daily sales, which helps them when they apply for a loan. Families living far apart can send money to each other at any hour, even on holidays. Bills for electricity, water, gas and mobile phones can be paid from home without standing in long queues. However, the new system also asks for new care. Fraudsters often try to trick people with fake calls and messages. They may pretend to be bank officers, delivery agents or even relatives in trouble. The most important rule is simple: never share your secret number or the one-time password with anyone, no matter who they claim to be. Remember that you never need to enter your secret number to receive money. It is also wise to check the name shown on the screen before confirming a payment and to keep the app updated. If something goes wrong, the bank should be informed at once, because quick action improves the chance of getting the money back. Cash has not disappeared, and many older people still prefer it. Both systems will probably exist side by side for a long time. What matters is that every user understands how to pay safely and teaches the same to others in the family. Farmers are often called the backbone of the country, and for good reason. The grain in our kitchens, the vegetables in the market and the milk on our table all depend on their hard work. Yet most of us know very little about the life of a farmer and the many risks he or she faces every season. A farmer's day begins before sunrise. There are animals to feed, fields to water and crops to watch over. The work changes with the season. At sowing time, the soil has to be ploughed and the seeds chosen with care. As the plants grow, they must be protected from weeds, insects and diseases. At harvest time, the whole family often joins in, and the work goes on from morning to night. Farming depends heavily on the weather. Too little rain can dry up the crop, while too much rain or a sudden hailstorm can destroy months of effort in a single afternoon. Prices in the market also go up and down, and a good harvest does not always mean a good income. Many small farmers own only a little land and have to borrow money to buy seeds, fertiliser and diesel. In recent years, new ideas have started to help. Drip irrigation saves water by delivering it straight to the roots. Soil testing tells the farmer which nutrients the land really needs, so that fertiliser is not wasted. Mobile phones bring weather alerts and market prices to the village. Farmers who form groups can buy inputs together and sell their produce at better rates. Respecting the farmer is not only a matter of words. We can avoid wasting food, buy directly from local growers when possible and support efforts that make farming a safer and more rewarding way of life.

स्किल टेस्ट में अक्सर क्या होता है?

बहुत सी सरकारी भर्तियों में, जैसे क्लर्क या डेटा एंट्री वाले पदों में, लिखित परीक्षा पास करने के बाद एक टाइपिंग स्किल टेस्ट होता है। अक्सर यह कंप्यूटर पर होता है: स्क्रीन पर एक अनुच्छेद दिखता है और नीचे एक बॉक्स में उसे टाइप करना होता है, जैसा इस पेज पर है। समय अक्सर 10 या 15 मिनट के आसपास होता है, और कई भर्तियों में यह टेस्ट सिर्फ़ क्वालिफ़ाइंग होता है — यानी पास होना ज़रूरी है, लेकिन इसके अंक अंतिम मेरिट में नहीं जुड़ते। किस भर्ती में कितना समय, कितनी स्पीड और कितनी ग़लती की छूट है, यह हर बार अधिसूचना में लिखा होता है; वही मानें।

स्पीड की सीमा और ग़लतियाँ

इंग्लिश के लिए अक्सर 35 WPM के आसपास और हिंदी के लिए 30 WPM के आसपास जैसी सीमाएँ सुनने को मिलती हैं, लेकिन इसे पक्का न मानें — हर भर्ती अपनी सीमा ख़ुद बताती है। कई जगह स्पीड कीस्ट्रोक से नापी जाती है, और ग़लतियों की एक तय प्रतिशत तक छूट होती है। कुछ ग़लतियाँ पूरी और कुछ आधी गिनी जा सकती हैं, जैसे स्पेसिंग या विराम चिह्न। यह टूल हर ग़लत, छूटे या फ़ालतू शब्द को पूरी ग़लती गिनता है, इसलिए यहाँ का नेट WPM थोड़ा सख़्त अंदाज़ा है — यह अच्छी बात है।

10 मिनट के टेस्ट की तैयारी

  1. पहले 5 मिनट के टेस्ट में लगातार अपनी लक्ष्य स्पीड पार करें।
  2. फिर 10 मिनट के टेस्ट दें और देखें कि आख़िरी मिनटों में स्पीड कितनी गिरती है।
  3. अगर शुद्धता 92–93% से नीचे जाए तो स्पीड कम करें; ग़लतियाँ अक्सर ज़्यादा नुक़सान करती हैं।
  4. हफ़्ते में दो बार बैकस्पेस बंद करके भी दें, ताकि सुधारने में समय बर्बाद करने की आदत छूटे।
  5. परीक्षा से पहले के हफ़्ते रोज़ एक ही समय पर टेस्ट दें, जैसे असली परीक्षा का समय हो।

परीक्षा के दिन के लिए काम की बातें

  • शुरू होने से पहले कुर्सी, कीबोर्ड और स्क्रीन की दूरी ठीक कर लें।
  • पहली पंक्ति पढ़कर शुरू करें; घबराहट में शुरुआत की ग़लतियाँ सबसे आम हैं।
  • एक शब्द ग़लत हो जाए तो आगे बढ़ें — कई बार पीछे जाकर सुधारने में ज़्यादा समय जाता है।
  • पंक्ति बदलते समय उँगली की जगह आँख से याद रखें कि कौन सा शब्द था।
  • कीबोर्ड नया या अलग हो सकता है; इसलिए अभ्यास में भी कभी-कभी दूसरे कीबोर्ड पर टाइप करें।

इस पेज का टूल

यहाँ 10 मिनट और इंग्लिश पहले से चुने हुए हैं, और बैकस्पेस चालू है। आप चाहें तो समय, भाषा या बैकस्पेस बदल सकते हैं। हर टेस्ट का परिणाम आपके ब्राउज़र में सेव होता है — किसी सर्वर पर नहीं — ताकि आप हफ़्ते-दर-हफ़्ते अपनी प्रगति देख सकें।

अक्सर पूछे जाने वाले सवाल

क्या यह असली SSC टेस्ट है?

नहीं। यह एक फ़्री अभ्यास टूल है जो स्किल टेस्ट जैसा माहौल देता है। असली टेस्ट का तरीक़ा, समय और नियम सिर्फ़ आधिकारिक अधिसूचना में होते हैं।

SSC जैसे टेस्ट में स्पीड कैसे नापी जाती है?

अक्सर कीस्ट्रोक के हिसाब से — जैसे 5 कीस्ट्रोक को एक शब्द माना जाता है — और ग़लतियों की एक सीमा तय होती है। सही तरीक़ा हर अधिसूचना में देखें।

हिंदी में स्किल टेस्ट का अभ्यास कैसे करें?

ऊपर भाषा में हिंदी चुन लें और 10 मिनट रखें। हिंदी के लिए डिवाइस में यूनिकोड हिंदी कीबोर्ड चाहिए।

10 मिनट में कितने शब्द टाइप करने होते हैं?

यह स्पीड पर निर्भर है: 35 WPM की स्पीड पर 10 मिनट में लगभग 1750 कीस्ट्रोक, यानी क़रीब 350 "शब्द" बनते हैं।

बैकस्पेस परीक्षा में चलता है?

यह हर टेस्ट के सॉफ़्टवेयर और नियम पर निर्भर है। इसलिए दोनों तरह अभ्यास करना अच्छा है — इस पेज पर बैकस्पेस चालू है, ऊपर से बंद कर सकते हैं।
